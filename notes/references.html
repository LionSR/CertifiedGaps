Spacetime Bootstrap References

A short list for the arXiv version of the Haldane-gap paper: only work the paper uses directly, or work that proves spectral gaps by closely related means. Each entry has a small figure of what that paper did and a line on why to cite it. Each citation was checked against its Crossref DOI record, its arXiv page, or the local copy of OpenAI's preprints.

Suggested disclaimer

The literature on spectral gaps of quantum spin chains is large, and this reference list is not a survey. We cite only work that we use directly or that proves spectral gaps by closely related means. Several related preprints appeared in 2026 while this work was being completed; we cite those we know of, and we apologize to authors whose relevant work we have not cited.

For a PRL Letter you can keep only the first two sentences and put the full version in the Supplemental Material.

What the gap implies

  1. [7]

    H. Tasaki, Ground state of the S=1 antiferromagnetic Heisenberg chain is topologically nontrivial if gapped, Phys. Rev. Lett. 134, 076602 (2025).

    Why cite: his hypothesis is about open chains with boundary fields, not periodic rings, and OpenAI’s boundary-field preprint [2] proves it (its Sec. 7 applies his theorem). So attribute the topological consequence to [2] together with [7]; a gap on periodic rings alone does not meet the hypothesis.

    Crossref ✓10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.076602arXiv:2407.17041
  2. [8]

    H. Tasaki, expository account of the proof of the spin-1 Haldane gap, in preparation (2026); title provisional.

    What it is: on 13 Oct 2026 Tasaki announced on X that, a week after learning of OpenAI's proof, he was "almost convinced the proof is valid" and was writing a "more human-readable account", with hand-drawn figures.

    Why cite: an independent expert reading of the same proof this paper reformulates. Cite it as in preparation now and replace it with the arXiv number once it appears.

    announced · not yet postedx.com/Hal_Tasaki

    Why cite: the paper's X_β is this continuous MPO, and the C ⊗ C̄ reflection-positivity condition is the general reason it is Hermitian.

    Why cite: rigorous spectral control of a spatial transfer matrix, but only at high temperature and for an integrable chain; the bootstrap needs low temperature and no integrability.

    Crossref ✓10.1007/s00220-020-03749-6

The spatial purity as a partition-function ratio

With imaginary time closed, the spatial purity Ps(n, β) = Z2n(β)/Zn(β)² is the inverse of Gu and Wen's ratio X₁. These papers use X₁ numerically to count degenerate sectors and locate critical points; none bounds it rigorously or couples it to the thermal purity, which is what the doubling step does.

  1. [15]

    Z.-C. Gu and X.-G. Wen, Tensor-entanglement-filtering renormalization approach and symmetry-protected topological order, Phys. Rev. B 80, 155131 (2009).

    Why cite: X₁ is the inverse spatial purity, used the same way to detect a unique dominant sector. The observable predates this paper; the rigorous doubling with the thermal purity does not.

    Crossref ✓10.1103/PhysRevB.80.155131
  2. [16]

    S. Morita and N. Kawashima, Tensor renormalization group calculations of partition-function ratios, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 95, 044001 (2026).

    Why cite: the clearest statement that the Gu–Wen ratio is a ratio of partition functions on a torus and its doubling, i.e. 1/Ps.

    Crossref ✓10.7566/JPSJ.95.044001
  3. [17]

    S. Akiyama, R. G. Jha, J. Maeda, Y. Tanizaki, and J. Unmuth-Yockey, Tensor renormalization group approach to critical phenomena via symmetry-twisted partition functions, Phys. Rev. D 113, 074502 (2026).

    Why cite: twisted partition functions to resolve symmetry sectors, as in the symmetry-sector inversion of the spin-1 certificate.

    Crossref ✓10.1103/ywhr-lt6w

Other ways to prove spectral gaps

Every gap method here except [25] needs a frustration-free Hamiltonian, one whose ground state minimizes each local term, and [25] is limited to qubits (spin ½). The spin-1 Heisenberg chain is neither, and the paper should state that contrast in one sentence. [26] needs neither assumption but bounds string order, not the gap.

  1. [18]

    S. Knabe, Energy gaps and elementary excitations for certain VBS-quantum antiferromagnets, J. Stat. Phys. 52, 627–638 (1988).

    Why cite: the original "small system implies every size" argument; this paper extends that idea to a frustrated chain.

    Crossref ✓10.1007/BF01019721
  2. [19]

    M. Fannes, B. Nachtergaele, and R. F. Werner, Finitely correlated states on quantum spin chains, Commun. Math. Phys. 144, 443–490 (1992).

    Why cite: MPS, transfer operators, and the martingale gap method in one place. [24] improves on it.

    Crossref ✓10.1007/BF02099178
  3. [20]

    D. Gosset and E. Mozgunov, Local gap threshold for frustration-free spin systems, J. Math. Phys. 57, 091901 (2016).

    Why cite: the sharp form of the finite-size criterion in one dimension.

    Crossref ✓10.1063/1.4962337
  4. [21]

    M. Lemm, A. W. Sandvik, and L. Wang, Existence of a spectral gap in the Affleck-Kennedy-Lieb-Tasaki model on the hexagonal lattice, Phys. Rev. Lett. 124, 177204 (2020).

    Why cite: computer-assisted gap proof, the closest precedent for this paper's style. Note that its numerical step is DMRG, not interval arithmetic.

    Crossref ✓10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.177204
  5. [22]

    N. Pomata and T.-C. Wei, Demonstrating the Affleck-Kennedy-Lieb-Tasaki spectral gap on 2D degree-3 lattices, Phys. Rev. Lett. 124, 177203 (2020).

    Why cite: independent computer-assisted proof of the same result as [21].

    Crossref ✓10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.177203
  6. [23]

    K. S. Rai, I. Kull, P. Emonts, J. Tura, N. Schuch, and F. Baccari, A hierarchy of spectral gap certificates for frustration-free spin systems, Quantum 10, 2065 (2026).

    Why cite: a strong recent finite-size gap certificate, from Schuch's group; it needs frustration-freeness.

    Crossref ✓10.22331/q-2026-04-13-2065arXiv:2411.03680
  7. [24]

    M. Á. Rozmán, A. Molnár, and N. Schuch, Lower bounds on spectral gaps of parent Hamiltonians via tensor networks, arXiv:2607.19078 (2026).

    Why cite: a second 2026 gap method from Schuch's group, this one using tensor networks, again for frustration-free models.

    arXiv ✓ · 2026 preprintarXiv:2607.19078
  8. [25]

    S. Rao, A convergent hierarchy of spectral gap certificates for qubit Hamiltonians, arXiv:2510.08427 (2025).

    Why cite: the nearest rival in scope, since it also handles frustrated Hamiltonians. Revised 2 Oct 2026.

    arXiv ✓ · preprintarXiv:2510.08427
  9. [26]

    S. Banerjee, H. Guo, and D. Chowdhury, Bootstrap certification of string order in quantum spin chains, arXiv:2609.22420 (2026).

    Why cite: concurrent certification on the same spin-1 chain (string order, not the gap). OpenAI's [1] cites it too.

    arXiv ✓ · 2026 preprintarXiv:2609.22420
  10. [27]

    T. S. Cubitt, D. Pérez-García, and M. M. Wolf, Undecidability of the spectral gap, Nature 528, 207–211 (2015).

    Why cite: explains why each gap needs its own certificate.

    Crossref ✓10.1038/nature16059
