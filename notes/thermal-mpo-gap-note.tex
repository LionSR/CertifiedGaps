\title{A Diagrammatic Proof of the Haldane Gap via Spacetime Tensor Networks}
\date{October 8, 2026}
\begin{abstract}
Following the recent machine-assisted resolution of the Haldane conjecture for periodic and boundary-field spin-1 Heisenberg chains by OpenAI~\cite{source,boundary}, we show that the analytical derivations can be recast as elementary tensor-network contractions.
Formulating the system as an exact continuous-time thermal matrix product operator exposes the underlying spacetime geometry.
The step from a finite-scale estimate to a uniform gap then reduces to a contractive scale-doubling recurrence on planar diagrams, under three pages long and formalized in Lean~4 with the two numerical inputs as its only unproved hypotheses.
The finite-scale estimate itself is taken unchanged from Ref.~\cite{source}, whose certificate uses spectral filters and character projections.
This illustrates how diagrammatic representations can clarify and compress machine-generated proofs in quantum many-body physics.
\end{abstract}

We consider the antiferromagnetic spin-1 Heisenberg chain with periodic
boundary conditions $\bm S_{L+1}=\bm S_1$ and $J=k_B=1$:
\begin{equation}
 H_L=\sum_{j=1}^L\bm S_j\cdot\bm S_{j+1},\qquad
 Z_L(\beta)=\Tr e^{-\beta H_L}.
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
All system sizes are even and at least four. Let
$E_0(L)\le E_1(L)\le\cdots$ include multiplicities and define the gap
$\Delta_L=E_1(L)-E_0(L)$, the step from the ground level to the first
excited level, \levels. For the Gibbs weights
$w_m=e^{-\beta E_m(L)}/Z_L(\beta)$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{w_1}{w_0}=e^{-\beta\Delta_L}\le e^{-c\beta}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Delta_L\ge c.
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
The ratio decays in $\beta$ like \decayglyph; the task is to make its
rate $c$ independent of $L$:
\begin{theorem}[Periodic gap~\cite{source}]\label{thm:gap}
For every even $L\ge2304$, the chain~\eqref{eq:model} has a unique ground
state and $\Delta_L>(\log20)/784\simeq0.003821$.
\end{theorem}

The spectral gap of the chain~\eqref{eq:model} was established by OpenAI~\cite{source} using machine-assisted proof search over a thirty-page analytical construction. That proof relies on coordinate expansions, degree-12 polynomial spectral filters, and character decompositions across dihedral symmetry sectors. Automated theorem provers naturally explore such symbolic coordinate representations, which can accumulate intermediate algebraic machinery over long deduction horizons.

Here, we show that the underlying mechanism is fundamentally geometric. Representing the partition function as a continuous-time spacetime tensor network makes the dual spatial transfer operator, reflection Hermiticity, and scale-doubling contraction immediately manifest. The derivation proceeds in three stages. First, an exact continuous-time thermal MPO yields a self-adjoint spatial transfer operator. Second, spacetime contraction identities couple spatial and thermal purities, while scale doubling quadratically suppresses purity defects under joint iteration; we call this self-improving iteration the \emph{spacetime bootstrap}. Third, $\ell^p$ moment monotonicity extends the bound from dyadic scales to all intermediate even lengths to establish Eq.~\eqref{eq:target}.

Every step below is a contraction of one tensor network: horizontal
legs \virtleg{} are virtual (space), vertical double legs \physleg{} are
physical (imaginary time), joined legs \tnchain[node=xvertex]{,} are
contracted, closed loops \tnchain[node=xvertex,ring]{{}} are
traces~\cite{tang,pirvu}, and a labeled box \tnchain{A} is a product of
tensors. Blue marks thermal objects \tikz[tn]{\node[gibbs]{$\rho$};} and red marks
spatial ones \tikz[tn]{\node[xop]{$\sigma$};}.
Appendix~\ref{app:open} applies the same networks to an open chain with
boundary fields, where the spacetime is a cylinder rather than a torus; it reuses every identity below and adds only boundary vectors.

\emph{Thermal MPO.---}
To obtain a real symmetric transfer operator, use the Cartesian spin
basis $s,s'\in\{x,y,z\}$. The matrices
$(S^\alpha)_{ss'}=-i\epsilon_{\alpha ss'}$ give real operators $G_\alpha=iS^\alpha$,
$\|G_\alpha\|=1$, and $-H_L=\sum_{j,\alpha}G_{\alpha,j}G_{\alpha,j+1}$.
Each bond event $(\tau,\alpha)$ in the exponential expansion inserts
$G_\alpha$ at both endpoints. Let $a,b$ record these events on the two bonds
adjacent to a site, with $0<\tau<\beta$. The local MPO tensor is
\begin{equation}
 \Wlegs=W_\beta^{ss'}(a,b)=
 \left\langle s\left|\mathcal T_\tau
 \prod_{(\tau,\alpha)\in a\cup b}G_\alpha\right|s'\right\rangle,
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T_\tau$ places later times to the left. Summing event
labels and integrating ordered times defines a measure $\mu_\beta$ of
mass $e^{3\beta}$, with unit mass on the empty history. Contracting $n$
tensors around the ring recovers the absolutely convergent exponential
series as an exact MPO on the virtual space $L^2(\mu_\beta)$, without bond
truncation~\cite{tang,pirvu}:
\begin{equation}
 e^{-\beta H_n}=\tnchain[node=mpo,legs,ring]{W,W,\cdots,W,W}.
 \label{eq:mpo}
\end{equation}
Tracing the physical index, \Wtrace${}={}$\Xvertex, gives the spatial
transfer kernel $X_\beta(a,b)=\sum_s W_\beta^{ss}(a,b)$.
It is real, symmetric, and bounded in absolute value by three, hence
self-adjoint and Hilbert--Schmidt on $L^2(\mu_\beta)$.
Closing the ring of transfer vertices gives, for even $n$,
\begin{equation}
 Z_n(\beta)=\tnchain[node=xvertex,ring]{,,\cdots,,}=\trv X_\beta^n=\sum_i|\lambda_i(\beta)|^n,
 \label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_i(\beta)$ are the real eigenvalues of $X_\beta$, ordered by
modulus, $|\lambda_0|\ge|\lambda_1|\ge\cdots$. Raising them
to the $n$th power concentrates the weight on the largest:
\specbars[mpored]{.5,.3,.2}${}\mapsto{}$\specbars[mpored]{.87,.11,.02} for $n=4$.

\emph{Purity and excited-state weight.---}
For a positive trace-one operator with eigenvalues $p_0\ge p_1\ge\cdots$, the purity obeys
\begin{equation}
 P=\sum_i p_i^2\le p_0\sum_i p_i=p_0.
 \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
Thus $1-p_0\le1-P$: a purity near one forces a spectrum like
\specbars{.86,.08,.04,.02} with a single dominant bar.
For the Gibbs state, $P>1/2$ implies a unique ground state, with
excited-state population $1-w_0\le1-P$.

To propagate this bound, cut the ring of Eq.~\eqref{eq:moment} at one bond,
\tnchain[node=xvertex,ring]{,\cdots,}${}\mapsto{}$\tnchain[node=xvertex]{,\cdots,},
and normalize both the transfer and Gibbs operators:
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_n(\beta)=\frac{\tnchain[node=xvertex]{,,\cdots,,}}{Z_n(\beta)}=\frac{X_\beta^n}{Z_n(\beta)},\qquad
 \rho_n(\beta)=\frac{e^{-\beta H_n}}{Z_n(\beta)}.
 \label{eq:states}
\end{equation}
Both operators are positive with unit trace; denote their eigenvalues by
$p_i$ and $w_m$, respectively. Their purities join two copies, along
space for $\sigma_n$ and along imaginary time for $\rho_n$. Drawing
$Z_n(\beta)$ as a spacetime torus \Zbox[.7]21{} of width $n$ and height $\beta$
(dashed edges identified), both are one ratio of tori:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Ps(n,\beta)&=\trv\sigma_n^2=\tnchain[ring]{\sigma_n,\sigma_n}
   =\frac{Z_{2n}(\beta)}{Z_n(\beta)^2}=\frac{\Zt41}{\Ztsq21},\\[2pt]
 \Pt(n,\beta)&=\Tr\rho_n^2\;=\tncolumn[ring]{\rho_n,\rho_n}
   =\frac{Z_n(2\beta)}{Z_n(\beta)^2}=\frac{\Zt22}{\Ztsq21}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:purities}
\end{equation}

\emph{Squaring.---}
Squaring $\sigma_n$ doubles the length; squaring $\rho_n$ doubles the
inverse temperature:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \tnchain{\sigma_n,\sigma_n}=\Ps(n,\beta)\,\tnchain{\sigma_{2n}},\\[2pt]
 \tncolumn{\rho_n,\rho_n}=\Pt(n,\beta)\,\tncolumn[item={minimum height=1.6em}]{\rho_n(2\beta)}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:squaring}
\end{equation}
Both replace the normalized eigenvalues $p_i$ by $p_i^2/P$, which
concentrates the spectrum: \specbars{.5,.3,.2}${}\mapsto{}$\specbars{.66,.24,.1}.
To bound the new purity $P'$, use $1-P=2\sum_{i<k}p_ip_k$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 1-P'=\frac{2\sum_{i<k}p_i^2p_k^2}{P^2}
 \le\frac{(1-P)^2}{2P^2}=f(1-P),\\
 f(z)=\frac{z^2}{2(1-z)^2}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:square}
\end{equation}
Dropping cross terms from $(\sum_{i<k}p_ip_k)^2$ gives the inequality.
The function $f$ increases on $[0,1)$, with $f(z)\sim z^2/2$: squaring
roughly squares the purity defect $1-P$, keeping it far below the
diagonal, \fcurve{} for $0\le z\le.3$.

\emph{Spacetime coupling identities.---}
While squaring independently doubles space ($n\to 2n$), \Zt21${}\mapsto{}$\Zt41, or imaginary time ($\beta\to 2\beta$), \Zt21${}\mapsto{}$\Zt22, establishing a uniform gap requires doubling both variables simultaneously.
Contracting four copies of the partition function $Z_n(\beta)$ in either spatial or temporal order yields the composite torus $Z_{2n}(2\beta)$.
The networks are drawn to scale; red arrows join copies in space, blue arrows in time, and each arrow carries the purity factor it multiplies by:
\begin{proofdiagram}

 % One n x beta torus = 2 x 1 cells; gaps close as copies are joined.

 \foreach \b in {0,1}{\tnpatch{5.4}{{2.6+\b*(\c+\g)}}{4}{1}{\c}}  % Z_2n(beta)^2
 \foreach \a in {0,1}{\tnpatch{{.6+\a*(2*\c+\g)}}{.5}{2}{2}{\c}}  % Z_n(2beta)^2
 \tnpatch{5.4}{.5}{4}{2}{\c}                                     % Z_2n(2beta)

\end{tikzpicture}
\end{proofdiagram}
Writing each purity as its ratio of tori, Eq.~\eqref{eq:purities}, the
two paths cancel to the same quotient:
\begin{equation}
 \underbrace{\frac{\Zt42}{\Ztsq22}}_{\Ps(n,2\beta)}
 \underbrace{\frac{\Ztsq22}{\Ztsq[4]21}}_{\Pt(n,\beta)^2}
 =\frac{\Zt42}{\Ztsq[4]21}=
 \underbrace{\frac{\Ztsq41}{\Ztsq[4]21}}_{\Ps(n,\beta)^2}
 \underbrace{\frac{\Zt42}{\Ztsq41}}_{\Pt(2n,\beta)}.
 \label{eq:cancel}
\end{equation}
Solving for one factor, and repeating at length $2n$, gives
\begin{align}
 \Ps(n,2\beta)&=\frac{\Ps(n,\beta)^2\Pt(2n,\beta)}{\Pt(n,\beta)^2},
 \label{eq:couple1}\\
 \Pt(4n,\beta)&=\frac{\Pt(2n,\beta)^2\Ps(2n,2\beta)}{\Ps(2n,\beta)^2}.
 \label{eq:couple2}
\end{align}

\emph{Doubling estimate.---}
Each application doubles the torus in both directions, \torusseq, and
roughly squares the purity defect. For even $n$ and $0\le\varepsilon\le1/100$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \Ps(n,\beta),\ \Pt(2n,\beta)\ge1-\varepsilon\\
 \Longrightarrow\quad
 \Ps(2n,2\beta),\ \Pt(4n,2\beta)\ge1-5\varepsilon^2.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
The scale-doubling update~\eqref{eq:update} is established by four moves, alternating
between spacetime coupling and operator squaring. Read the diagram below from top to
bottom: each box is one bound, red for $\Ps$ and blue for $\Pt$; solid boxes are given
or proved, dashed boxes intermediate. Each move is a numbered circle labeled by the
identity it uses; the grey arrow entering a circle is the second bound that move needs:
\begin{proofdiagram}
\begin{prooflanes}
 \lanebound{s}{s0}{0}{0}{$\Ps(n,\beta)\ge1-\varepsilon$}
 \lanebound{t}{t0}{1}{0}{$\Pt(2n,\beta)\ge1-\varepsilon$}
 \lanebound{so}{s1}{0}{1}{$\Ps(n,2\beta)\ge1-3\varepsilon$}
 \lanebound{s}{s2}{0}{2}{$\Ps(2n,2\beta)\ge1-5\varepsilon^2$}
 \lanebound{to}{t1}{1}{3}{$\Pt(4n,\beta)\ge1-3\varepsilon$}
 \lanebound{t}{t2}{1}{4}{$\Pt(4n,2\beta)\ge1-5\varepsilon^2$}
 \lanemove{s0}{s1}{1}{\eqref{eq:couple1}} \laneuse{t0}{1}
 \lanemove[mpored]{s1}{s2}{2}{square}
 \lanemove{t0}{t1}{3}{\eqref{eq:couple2}} \laneuse{s2}{3}
 \lanemove[mpoblue]{t1}{t2}{4}{square}
 \lanetag[above]{s0}{given} \lanetag[above]{t0}{given}
 \lanetag[below]{s2}{proved} \lanetag{t2}{proved}
\end{prooflanes}
\end{proofdiagram}
The proved pair is the given pair with $(n,\beta)\to(2n,2\beta)$, so it is
the given pair of the next scale, which is why the estimate can be iterated.

\emph{Move 1} (coupling). Since $\Pt(n,\beta)\le1$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:couple1} gives $\Ps(n,2\beta)\ge(1-\varepsilon)^3\ge1-3\varepsilon$.
\emph{Move 2} (squaring in space). For even $n$, $\sigma_n(2\beta)$ has
nonnegative spectrum and squares to $\sigma_{2n}(2\beta)$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:square} and monotonicity of $f$ give $\Ps(2n,2\beta)\ge1-\delta$, where
\begin{equation}
 \delta=f\bigl(1-(1-\varepsilon)^3\bigr)
 =\tfrac12\bigl[(1-\varepsilon)^{-3}-1\bigr]^2
 \le5\varepsilon^2\le\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
Here $1-(1-\varepsilon)^3\le3\varepsilon$ and
$9/[2(1-3\varepsilon)^2]\le5$ for $\varepsilon\le1/100$.
\emph{Move 3} (coupling). Eq.~\eqref{eq:couple2} with
$\Ps(2n,\beta)\le1$ and $\delta\le\varepsilon$ gives
$\Pt(4n,\beta)\ge(1-\varepsilon)^2(1-\delta)\ge(1-\varepsilon)^3$.
\emph{Move 4} (squaring in time). $\rho_{4n}(\beta)$ squares to
$\rho_{4n}(2\beta)$, so $\Pt(4n,2\beta)\ge1-\delta\ge1-5\varepsilon^2$.
This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:update}.

\emph{Finite input and iteration.---}
Proposition~5.5 of Ref.~\cite{source} provides the base-scale estimates:
\begin{equation}
 \Ps(2304,784),\ \Pt(4608,784)\ge0.99.
 \label{eq:input}
\end{equation}
The energy shift used there cancels from the purity ratios.
After $j\ge0$ doublings, set
\begin{equation}
 n_j=2304\,2^j,\qquad \beta_j=784\,2^j,\qquad
 \varepsilon_j=\frac{20^{-2^j}}5.
 \label{eq:scales}
\end{equation}
Since $\varepsilon_0=1/100$ and $\varepsilon_{j+1}=5\varepsilon_j^2$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:update} gives
$\Ps(n_j,\beta_j),\Pt(2n_j,\beta_j)\ge1-\varepsilon_j$ by induction on $j$:
each application runs the four moves above once.
The defect falls doubly exponentially in $j$,
$\varepsilon_0=10^{-2}$\,\sparkline{-22}{-2}{-2,-3.30,-5.90,-11.11,-21.52}\,$\varepsilon_4\approx3\times10^{-22}$ (log scale),
and so exponentially in inverse temperature,
$\varepsilon_j=\frac15\exp[-\beta_j(\log20)/784]$, as needed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:target}.

These estimates hold at the doubled lengths. To cover every even
$L\ge2304$, choose $j$ with $n_j\le L\le2n_j$.
Monotonicity of the $\ell^p$ norms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:moment} gives
\begin{align}
 Z_L(\beta_j)&\le Z_{n_j}(\beta_j)^{L/n_j},\notag\\
 Z_L(2\beta_j)&\ge Z_{2n_j}(2\beta_j)^{L/(2n_j)}.
 \label{eq:moments}
\end{align}
Substitution into the thermal-purity ratio gives
\begin{align}
 \Pt(L,\beta_j)
 &\ge\bigl[\Pt(2n_j,\beta_j)\Ps(n_j,\beta_j)^2\bigr]^{L/(2n_j)}\notag\\
 &\ge(1-\varepsilon_j)^3\ge1-3\varepsilon_j>\tfrac12.
 \label{eq:interpolate}
\end{align}
Here $L/(2n_j)\le1$, and both purities in brackets are at least
$1-\varepsilon_j$.

\emph{Recovering the gap.---}
Order the eigenvalues of $\rho_L(\beta_j)$ as $w_0\ge w_1\ge\cdots$.
By Eqs.~\eqref{eq:weight} and \eqref{eq:interpolate},
$w_0\ge1-3\varepsilon_j>1/2$: the Gibbs spectrum looks like
\specbars{.97,.02,.01}, with the ground state alone above one half.
Two ground states would show two equal top bars, \specbars{.49,.49,.02},
neither above one half; so the ground state is unique. The remaining weight bounds $w_1$, and hence
\begin{equation}
 e^{-\beta_j\Delta_L}=\frac{w_1}{w_0}
 \le\frac{1-w_0}{w_0}
 \le\frac{3\varepsilon_j}{1-3\varepsilon_j}
 <20^{-2^j}.
 \label{eq:gapratio}
\end{equation}
The last inequality uses
$3\varepsilon_j/(1-3\varepsilon_j)\le(60/97)20^{-2^j}$.
Taking logarithms and dividing by $\beta_j=784\,2^j$ gives, for every
even $L\ge2304$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Delta_L>\frac{\log20}{784}\simeq0.003821.}
 \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, conditional on Eq.~\eqref{eq:input};
in particular $\liminf_{L\to\infty,\;L\,\mathrm{even}}\Delta_L\ge(\log20)/784>0$.
The scale-doubling recurrence and the gap deduction are formally verified in Lean~4 (\path{lean/CertifiedGaps/PurityCriterion.lean}).


\section*{Discussion: Representation in Machine-Assisted Proofs}
The uniform spectral gap theorems for the periodic and boundary-field spin-1 Heisenberg chains, together with the certified finite-scale numerical inputs, originate from the machine-assisted proofs by OpenAI~\cite{source,boundary}.

The original thirty-page proofs isolate eigenvalues and bridge scales through extensive coordinate constructions, including degree-12 polynomial spectral filters and dihedral character projectors, alongside lemmas that remain unused in the final inequality. Automated reasoning systems typically operate via local symbolic rewrite rules in a fixed basis. Without the geometric and topological representations native to many-body physics, long-horizon symbolic search tends to accumulate representational overhead to ensure soundness.

In this work, the authors proposed to GPT-6 to examine the argument through spacetime tensor networks, and interactively digested the resulting structure. Tensor network diagrams provide the geometric inductive bias that symbolic coordinate expansions lack. By placing imaginary time and spatial extent on equal footing on a Euclidean torus, the self-adjoint spatial transfer operator and contractive scale-doubling iteration become elementary planar identities. This indicates that diagrammatic tensor networks can serve not only as a calculational tool, but as an effective distillation framework for interpreting, simplifying, and extracting the essential physical mechanisms from machine-generated mathematical proofs. All mathematical arguments and formal proofs were verified and adjudicated by the authors.

\section{Open chain with boundary fields}\label{app:open}
The networks above also bound the gap of the open chain with equal
endpoint fields. On $n$ spin-1 sites, with $\sum_\alpha(S^\alpha)^2=2I$, let
\begin{equation}
 H_n^{\same}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\bm S_i\cdot\bm S_{i+1}
       -h(S_1^z+S_n^z),\qquad h=\frac35.
 \label{eq:b-model}
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Boundary-field gap~\cite{boundary}]\label{thm:b-gap}
For every odd $n\ge1921$, the Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:b-model} has a unique
ground state and $\Delta_n>(\log10)/392\simeq0.005874$.
\end{theorem}
Only one ingredient is new. A periodic network is closed by a trace,
\tnchain[node=xvertex,ring]{,\cdots,}, which always contains the leading transfer
eigenvector; an open chain is closed by two boundary vectors,
\tnchain[node=xvertex,caps]{,\cdots,}, which could be orthogonal to it. The proof therefore
tracks this \emph{boundary overlap} alongside the thermal purity. As in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:target} and \eqref{eq:weight}, it suffices to bound the
equal-field thermal purity $\Pt^{\same}(n,\beta)=\sum_mw_m^2$ from below, since
\begin{equation}
 e^{-\beta\Delta_n}=\frac{w_1}{w_0}
 \le\frac{1-\Pt^{\same}(n,\beta)}{\Pt^{\same}(n,\beta)}.
 \label{eq:b-target}
\end{equation}
The scales of this appendix replace those of Eq.~\eqref{eq:scales}; they are
fixed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-scales}, and $\varepsilon_j$ denotes the periodic
purity defect of Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-tail}.

\emph{Boundary vectors.---}
The open chain uses the tensor $W_\beta$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:local}, with the
energy shift $\kappa=700741/500000$ of Ref.~\cite{boundary} (called $a$ there)
absorbed as a factor $e^{-\kappa\beta}$, i.e.\ $H_n^\nu\to H_n^\nu+\kappa nI$;
it cancels from every Gibbs state. Opening the ring of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mpo}
and capping it with boundary vectors gives
\begin{equation}
 e^{-\beta H_n^{\same}}=\tnchain[node=mpo,legs,capl=\bar v_\beta,capr=U_Pv_\beta]{W,W,\cdots,W,W}.
 \label{eq:b-mpo}
\end{equation}
For a history $a$ of $d$ bond events, the boundary vector is
\begin{equation}
 v_\beta(a)=
 \begin{cases}(ih)^d,&\text{all labels are }z,\\0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \|v_\beta\|^2=e^{\beta h^2},
 \label{eq:b-vector}
\end{equation}
with value one on the empty history. Each event on a cap inserts a field
on the end site: the left cap is conjugated, so its factor
$(-ih)G_z=+hS^z$ adds $-hS_1^z$ to $H$, while the same vector on the right,
$(ih)G_z=-hS^z$, adds $+hS_n^z$. The $\pi$ rotation $P=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)$
about $x$ flips every $z$ label, so its virtual action $U_P$ reverses the right
field. The right cap alone therefore sets the field pattern of the trace:
\begin{align}
 Y_n(\beta)&=\Tr e^{-\beta H_n^{\opp}}
 =\tnchain[capl={\bar v_\beta},capr={v_\beta}]{X_\beta^n},\quad\fieldopp,\label{eq:b-y}\\
 Y_n^{\same}(\beta)&=\Tr e^{-\beta H_n^{\same}}
 =\tnchain[capl={\bar v_\beta},capr={U_Pv_\beta}]{X_\beta^n},\quad\fieldsame,
 \label{eq:b-ys}
\end{align}
so $H_n^{\opp}=H_n^{\same}+2hS_n^z$; here $X_\beta^n$ is the chain of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:states}, and $Z_n$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:moment} is the periodic
counterpart. The opposite-field chain has identical bra and ket caps, so
its even moments are nonnegative. Simultaneous spin rotations of the
histories act on $L^2(\mu_\beta)$ as real orthogonal operators commuting with
$X_\beta$; in particular $[U_P,X_\beta]=0$. Before the shift, the absolute
traced terms sum to at most $3^n e^{\beta[3(n-1)+2h]}$, which justifies the
regrouping.

\emph{Transfer operator spectrum.---}
At successive inverse temperatures $\beta_{j+1}=2\beta_j$, the periodic estimates show that the leading eigenvalue of Eq.~\eqref{eq:moment}, $\lambda_j:=\lambda_0(\beta_j)$, is simple and positive. Normalizing it to one:
\begin{equation}
 T_j:=X_{\beta_j}/\lambda_j=\Pi_j+R_j,\qquad
 \Pi_j=|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|,\quad\|\psi_j\|=1,
 \label{eq:b-split}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_jR_j=R_j\Pi_j=0$, so $T_j^m=\Pi_j+R_j^m$.
Furthermore, $\|R_j\|<1$, $U_P\Pi_j=\Pi_j$, and $0<r_j:=\lambda_{j+1}/\lambda_j^2\le1$ (Appendix~\ref{app:b-periodic}).

\emph{Boundary overlap and residual decomposition.---}
Put $v_j=v_{\beta_j}$. Inserting the leading projector disconnects the network:
\begin{equation}
 \underbrace{\tnchain[capl={\bar v_j},capr={v_j}]{\Pi_j}}_{c_j}
 =\tnchain[capl=\bar v_j,nostubr]{\psi_j}\;\;\tnchain[nostubl,capr=v_j]{\bar\psi_j}
 =|\langle\psi_j,v_j\rangle|^2.
 \label{eq:b-overlap}
\end{equation}
The overlap $c_j$ is the squared projection of $v_j$ onto $\psi_j$,
\overlapglyph; unlike a periodic trace, where $\trv\Pi_j=1$, it vanishes when
the two are orthogonal. For even $m\ge2$,
\begin{equation}
 \underbrace{\tnchain[capl={\bar v_j},capr={v_j}]{T_j^m}}_{B_m(j)}
 =\underbrace{\tnchain[capl={\bar v_j},capr={v_j}]{\Pi_j}}_{c_j}
 +\underbrace{\tnchain[capl={\bar v_j},capr={v_j}]{R_j^m}}_{D_m(j)}.
 \label{eq:b-boundary-split}
\end{equation}
Folding establishes nonnegativity of the remainder:
\begin{equation}
 D_m(j)=\folded{R_j^{m/2}}=\|R_j^{m/2}v_j\|^2\ge0.
 \label{eq:b-fold}
\end{equation}
Self-adjointness of $R_j$ makes the lower row the mirror of the upper one, while $B_m=Y_m/\lambda_j^m>0$.
The boundary defect is defined by
\begin{equation}
 e_m(j):=\frac{D_m(j)}{B_m(j)}=1-\frac{c_j}{B_m(j)},
 \label{eq:b-e}
\end{equation}
the grey share of the contraction, \stackbar{.14}, drawn at the starting
value $e_{142}(2)\le.14$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-seeds}. Any $e_m<1$ guarantees a
nonzero overlap with the leading eigenvector.

\emph{Spatial remainder suppression.---}
For even $m\ge k$, absorbing $k/2$ residual factors into each boundary gives $w=R_j^{k/2}v_j$ and
\begin{equation}
 D_m(j)=\tnchain[capl={\bar w},capr={w}]{R_j^{m-k}}
 \le\|R_j\|^{m-k}\underbrace{\|w\|^2}_{D_k(j)}.
 \label{eq:b-residual}
\end{equation}
Spatial growth suppresses the remainder geometrically, by $\|R_j\|^{m-k}$
\decayglyph[black!50], while leaving $c_j$ invariant, ensuring $c_j\le B_m(j)\le B_k(j)$.

\emph{Thermal operator composition.---}
Composition in imaginary time, $e^{-\beta H_m^{\opp}}e^{-\beta H_m^{\opp}}=e^{-2\beta H_m^{\opp}}$, couples temperatures:
\begin{equation}
 \underbrace{\stackedmpo}_{e^{-\beta H_m^{\opp}}\,e^{-\beta H_m^{\opp}}}
 =\underbrace{\tnchain[node=mpo,legs=1.9em,caps,item={minimum height=1.3em}]{W_{2\beta},W_{2\beta},\cdots,W_{2\beta}}}_{e^{-2\beta H_m^{\opp}}}.
 \label{eq:b-stack-mpo}
\end{equation}
This holds as an operator identity on the physical Hilbert space without requiring sitewise virtual fusion.
Normalizing the trace defines the opposite-field thermal purity:
\begin{equation}
 \Pt^{\opp}(m,\beta_j):=\frac{Y_m(\beta_{j+1})}{Y_m(\beta_j)^2}=\Tr\rho_m^{\opp}(\beta_j)^2\in(0,1],
 \label{eq:b-a}
\end{equation}
with $\rho_m^{\opp}(\beta)=e^{-\beta H_m^{\opp}}/Y_m(\beta)$.
Factoring out leading eigenvalues links adjacent temperatures:
\begin{equation}
 B_m(j+1)=\Pt^{\opp}(m,\beta_j)r_j^{-m}B_m(j)^2.
 \label{eq:b-bridge}
\end{equation}

\emph{Coupled overlap recurrence.---}
Assuming $e_k(j)<1$ at scale $j$, let primes denote $j+1$, choose even $m>k\ge2$, and let $\|R_{j+1}\|^{m-k}\le q<1$.
Spatial suppression at $j+1$ gives $D_m'\le qD_k'$.
Since $c'$ is length-independent, $D_k'-D_m'=B_k'-B_m'$, implying
\begin{equation}
 (1-q)D_m'\le q(B_k'-B_m'),\qquad
 e_m'\le\frac{q}{1-q}\left(\frac{B_k'}{B_m'}-1\right).
 \label{eq:b-new-e}
\end{equation}
Composition relates the ratio to the previous temperature:
\begin{align}
 \frac{B_k'}{B_m'}
 &=\frac{\Pt^{\opp}(k,\beta_j)}{\Pt^{\opp}(m,\beta_j)}\left[\frac{B_k(j)}{B_m(j)}\right]^2r_j^{m-k}\notag\\
 &\le\frac{1}{\Pt^{\opp}(m,\beta_j)[1-e_k(j)]^2},
 \label{eq:b-new-ratio}
\end{align}
using $\Pt^{\opp}(k,\beta_j),r_j\le1$ and $B_m(j)\ge c_j=[1-e_k(j)]B_k(j)>0$.
Because only the previous overlap appears in the denominator, $e_m'<1$ establishes that the updated overlap remains positive.
When $e_k(j)$ and $1-\Pt^{\opp}(m,\beta_j)$ are $\mathcal O(x)$, choosing $m-k$ with $q\le\mathcal O(x)$ ensures that $e_m'$ contracts quadratically as $\mathcal O(x^2)$.

\emph{Thermal purity propagation.---}
The boundary bound enables physical length doubling. For even $M$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-bridge} yields
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Pt^{\opp}(2M,\beta_j)}{\Pt^{\opp}(M,\beta_j)}
 =r_j^M\underbrace{\frac{B_{2M}(j+1)}{B_M(j+1)}}_{\displaystyle\ge1-e_M(j+1)}
       \underbrace{\left[\frac{B_M(j)}{B_{2M}(j)}\right]^2}_{\displaystyle\ge1}.
 \label{eq:b-coupling}
\end{equation}
The first bound retains $c_{j+1}$, while the second uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-residual}.
Because $r_j^M\approx1$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-ratios}], purity remains controlled upon doubling length.
Squaring the Gibbs operator advances temperature, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:squaring}:
\begin{equation}
 \tncolumn{\rho_m^{\opp},\rho_m^{\opp}}\;=\;\Pt^{\opp}(m,\beta_j)\,\tncolumn[item={minimum height=1.6em}]{\rho_m^{\opp}(\beta_{j+1})},
 \label{eq:stack}
\end{equation}
so the squaring bound~\eqref{eq:square} at $m=2M$ drives $1-\Pt^{\opp}$ to $\mathcal O(x^2)$.

\emph{Scale induction.---}
Coupling the overlap and purity recurrences enables simultaneous induction over physical length and inverse temperature.
Using the scales of this appendix,
\begin{equation}
 \beta_j=49\,2^{j-2},\quad n_j=120\,2^{j-2},\quad
 M_j=2n_j\quad(j\ge2),
 \label{eq:b-scales}
\end{equation}
with $k_2=142$, $k_{j+1}=M_j$, and with periodic error $\varepsilon_j$ bounded in Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-tail}, induction establishes
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \varepsilon_j,\ e_{k_j}(j),\ 1-\Pt^{\opp}(M_j,\beta_j)\le x_j\quad(j\ge5),\\
 x_5=.01,\qquad x_{j+1}=10x_j^2,\qquad
 x_j=10^{-1-2^{j-5}}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:b-x}
\end{equation}
For $j\ge3$, $k_j=M_j/2$, leaving $M_j/2$ sites to suppress remainders.
One step $j\to j+1$ is three moves. In the diagram below, read from top to
bottom, each box is one bound: grey for the boundary defect $e_k(j)$, blue
for the opposite-field thermal purity $\Pt^{\opp}$. Solid boxes are given at
scale $j$ or proved at scale $j+1$, the dashed box is intermediate, and the
grey arrow entering a move is the second bound it uses; the bounds shown hold
for $j\ge5$ (Appendix~\ref{app:b-iteration}):
\begin{proofdiagram}
\begin{prooflanes}
 \lanebound{b}{e0}{0}{0}{$e_{M_j/2}(j)\le x$}
 \lanebound{t}{t0}{1}{0}{$\Pt^{\opp}(M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-x$}
 \lanebound{b}{e1}{0}{1}{$e_{M_j}(j{+}1)\le10x^2$}
 \lanebound{to}{t1}{1}{2}{$\Pt^{\opp}(2M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-2x-16x^2$}
 \lanebound{t}{t2}{1}{3}{$\Pt^{\opp}(2M_j,\beta_{j+1})\ge1-10x^2$}
 \lanemove{e0}{e1}{1}{\eqref{eq:b-new-e}} \laneuse{t0}{1}
 \lanemove{t0}{t1}{2}{\eqref{eq:b-coupling}} \laneuse{e1}{2}
 \lanemove[mpoblue]{t1}{t2}{3}{square}
 \lanetag[above]{e0}{given} \lanetag[above]{t0}{given}
 \lanetag[below]{e1}{proved} \lanetag{t2}{proved}
\end{prooflanes}
\end{proofdiagram}
Move~1 is the overlap recurrence~\eqref{eq:b-new-e}--\eqref{eq:b-new-ratio},
move~2 the length doubling~\eqref{eq:b-coupling},
and move~3 the squaring~\eqref{eq:stack}. Since $M_{j+1}=2M_j$, the proved
pair is the given pair of scale $j+1$, so the step repeats. The defect falls doubly exponentially,
$x_5=10^{-2}$\,\sparkline{-17}{-2}{-2,-3,-5,-9,-17}\,$x_9=10^{-17}$ (log scale); since
$\beta_j=392\,2^{j-5}$, it decays exponentially in inverse temperature.

\emph{Equal boundary fields and odd lengths.---}
The opposite-field estimates extend to the equal-field Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:b-model} via the spin-rotation symmetry $U_P$.
Next to the leading projector, the rotation operator acts trivially:
\begin{equation}
 \tnchain[last=sym]{\Pi_j,U_P}\;=\;\tnchain{\Pi_j}.
 \label{eq:b-twist}
\end{equation}
Thus $Q_j(n):=Y_n^{\same}(\beta_j)/\lambda_j^n$ shares the leading value $c_j$.
For any even $M\le n$, commuting $U_P$ through the right-hand tensors ($[U_P,R_j]=0$) isolates the remainder:
\begin{align}
 Q_j(n)-c_j&=\tnchain[capl=\bar v_j,capr=v_j]{R_j^{M/2},R_j^{n-M}U_P,R_j^{M/2}}\notag\\
 &=\tnchain[capl={\bar w},capr={w}]{R_j^{n-M}U_P},\qquad
 w=R_j^{M/2}v_j.
 \label{eq:b-signed}
\end{align}
Because $\|R_j^{n-M}U_P\|\le1$,
\begin{equation}
 |Q_j(n)-c_j|\le\|w\|^2=D_M(j),
 \label{eq:b-odd-bound}
\end{equation}
bounding the odd-length remainder by $D_M(j)$ without requiring positivity of $R_j^n$.

For $M_j\le n\le2M_j$ and $M=M_j=k_{j+1}$, this yields
\begin{equation}
 Q_j(n)\le B_M(j),\quad
 Q_{j+1}(n)\ge(1-2x_{j+1})B_M(j+1)>0.
 \label{eq:b-odd-new}
\end{equation}
Dividing these contractions via Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-bridge} gives the equal-field purity:
\begin{align}
 \Pt^{\same}(n,\beta_j)
 &=\frac{Y_n^{\same}(\beta_{j+1})}{Y_n^{\same}(\beta_j)^2}
 \ge(1-2x_{j+1})\Pt^{\opp}(M,\beta_j)r_j^{n-M}\notag\\
 &\ge(1-2x_{j+1})(1-x_j)(1-\varepsilon_j)(1-\varepsilon_{j+1})\notag\\
 &\ge1-2x_j-26x_j^2\ge1-4x_j,
 \label{eq:b-final-purity}
\end{align}
using $r_j^{n-M}\ge r_j^M$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-ratios}] and Appendix~\ref{app:b-iteration} bounds ($\varepsilon_j\le x_j$, $\varepsilon_{j+1}\le6x_j^2$, $x_{j+1}=10x_j^2$).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:b-gap}]
The intervals $[M_j,2M_j]$ cover every $n\ge M_5=1920$.
For odd $n\ge1921$, choose such a $j$.
The largest Gibbs weight satisfies $w_0\ge\Pt^{\same}(n,\beta_j)\ge1-4x_j>1/2$:
the spectrum looks like \specbars{.97,.02,.01}, so the ground state is unique.
Equation~\eqref{eq:b-target} gives
\begin{equation}
 e^{-\beta_j\Delta_n}\le\frac{4x_j}{1-4x_j}
 \le\frac{5}{12}\,10^{-2^{j-5}}<10^{-2^{j-5}}.
 \label{eq:b-gap-final}
\end{equation}
Taking logarithms and using $\beta_j=392\,2^{j-5}$ yields $\Delta_n>(\log10)/392$.
\end{proof}

\section{Transfer bounds at the open-chain scales}\label{app:b-periodic}
For the scales in Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-scales}, Appendix~\ref{app:open} uses
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \|R_j\|^{n_j}\le d(\varepsilon_j),\qquad d(\varepsilon)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2-3\varepsilon},\\
 \varepsilon_2=.052,\qquad \varepsilon_{j+1}=6\varepsilon_j^2,
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:b-tail}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 0<r_j\le1,\quad r_j^{M_j}\ge(1-\varepsilon_j)(1-\varepsilon_{j+1}),\quad
 U_P\Pi_j=\Pi_j.
 \label{eq:b-ratios}
\end{equation}
These follow from the first row of Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-seeds} by the doubling
estimate of the main text, with a larger starting defect: if
$\Ps(n,\beta),\Pt(2n,\beta)\ge1-\varepsilon$, move~1 of Eq.~\eqref{eq:update}
gives $\Ps(n,2\beta)\ge(1-\varepsilon)^3$, and squaring with Eq.~\eqref{eq:square} bounds $1-\Ps(2n,2\beta)$ by
\begin{equation}
 f(1-(1-\varepsilon)^3)=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}
 \left[\sum_{s=1}^3(1-\varepsilon)^{-s}\right]^2\le6\varepsilon^2
 \label{eq:b-periodic-six}
\end{equation}
for $0\le\varepsilon\le.052$, in place of $5\varepsilon^2$. Since $6\varepsilon^2\le\varepsilon$,
moves~3 and 4 give the same bound for $1-\Pt(4n,2\beta)$.
Induction therefore proves
$\Ps(n_j,\beta_j),\Pt(M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-\varepsilon_j$.

Consider a probability list with purity at least $1-\varepsilon>1/2$ and
largest weight $p_0$; put $z=1-p_0$. The purity is at most
$p_0^2+z^2=1-2z(1-z)$, so $2z(1-z)\le\varepsilon$. It is also at most $p_0$,
so $p_0>1/2$: the largest weight is unique and $z<1/2$. Then
$z\le2z(1-z)\le\varepsilon$, hence $z\le\varepsilon/[2(1-\varepsilon)]$ and $z/(1-z)\le d(\varepsilon)$.
Applied to the eigenvalues $p_i=|\lambda_i(\beta_j)|^{n_j}/Z_{n_j}(\beta_j)$ of $\sigma_{n_j}(\beta_j)$,
the largest-modulus eigenvalue is simple and
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i\ge1}\left|\frac{\lambda_i(\beta_j)}{\lambda_j}\right|^{n_j}
 \le d(\varepsilon_j),
 \label{eq:b-full-tail}
\end{equation}
which implies Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-tail}.
Positivity of $Z_N(\beta_j)$ for all large odd $N$ ensures that the leading eigenvalue is positive, since a negative leading eigenvalue would make $\trv X_{\beta_j}^N$ negative.
The residual sum tends to zero since $X_{\beta_j}$ is Hilbert--Schmidt
and its residual spectral radius is strictly smaller than $\lambda_j$.

The real kernel admits real orthogonal virtual actions of the three
$\pi$ rotations and the cyclic permutation of the coordinate axes,
all commuting with $X_{\beta_j}$. On its real one-dimensional leading
eigenspace these actions are signs. The order-three cyclic rotation
has sign $+1$ and conjugates the three $\pi$ rotations into one another,
so their signs agree. The product of two is the third; their common
sign is therefore $+1$. This proves $U_P\Pi_j=\Pi_j$.

Finally $Z_N(\beta_{j+1})\le Z_N(\beta_j)^2$ by physical purity.
Taking $N$th roots along even $N\to\infty$ gives
$\lambda_{j+1}\le\lambda_j^2$. For the lower bound at the required
finite length, the largest eigenvalue of $\sigma_{M_j}(\beta_{j+1})$
is at least $1-\varepsilon_{j+1}$, since $M_j=n_{j+1}$. Hence
\begin{align*}
 \lambda_{j+1}^{M_j}
 &\ge(1-\varepsilon_{j+1})Z_{M_j}(\beta_{j+1})\\
 &\ge(1-\varepsilon_{j+1})(1-\varepsilon_j)Z_{M_j}(\beta_j)^2\\
 &\ge(1-\varepsilon_{j+1})(1-\varepsilon_j)\lambda_j^{2M_j},
\end{align*}
which is the remaining bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-ratios}.


\section{Starting estimates for the open chain}\label{app:b-iteration}
The certified initial estimates~\eqref{eq:b-seeds} combine periodic variational estimates with small-cluster boundary certificates from Ref.~\cite{boundary}:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \Ps(120,49)\ge .948,\qquad \Pt(240,49)\ge .948,\\
 e_{142}(2)\le .14,\qquad \Pt^{\opp}(240,\beta_{2})\ge .79.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:b-seeds}
\end{equation}

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:b-new-e} and \eqref{eq:b-new-ratio} gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 e_m(j+1)\le F(q,e_k(j),\Pt^{\opp}(m,\beta_j)),\\
 F(q,e,t)=\frac{q}{1-q}\left[\frac1{(1-e)^2t}-1\right].
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:b-F}
\end{equation}
This function increases with $q,e$ and decreases with $t$.
Track $e_{k_j}(j)\le E_j$ and $\Pt^{\opp}(M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-\eta_j$.
With $k_2=142$ and $k_{j+1}=M_j$, the updates are
\begin{align}
 q_j&=d(\varepsilon_{j+1})^{(M_j-k_j)/M_j},\label{eq:b-q}\\
 E_{j+1}&\ge F(q_j,E_j,1-\eta_j),\label{eq:b-E-update}\\
 \eta_{j+1}&\ge f\bigl(1-(1-\eta_j)(1-\varepsilon_j)
                   (1-\varepsilon_{j+1})(1-E_{j+1})\bigr).
 \label{eq:b-eta-update}
\end{align}
Choose $E_{j+1},\eta_{j+1}$ in $[0,1)$.
Here $q_j$ follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-tail} with $n_{j+1}=M_j$, while Eqs.~\eqref{eq:b-coupling} and \eqref{eq:square} determine the purity update.
Thus $\varepsilon_2=.052$, $E_2=.14$, and $\eta_2=.21$ start the iteration.
Three applications of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:b-E-update}--\eqref{eq:b-eta-update}
give the following rational upper bounds:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
$j$ & $E_j$ & $\eta_j$ & $q_j$ (upper bound)\\
\midrule
2 & .14 & .21 & .142\\
3 & .12 & .155 & .0282\\
4 & .016 & .026 & .0028\\
5 & .001 & .001 & ---\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both defects collapse along the iteration, $E_j$:
\specbars[black!45]{1,.86,.11,.007} and $\eta_j$: \specbars{1,.74,.12,.005}
($j=2,\dots,5$, each relative to $j=2$).
Rational arithmetic confirms these step bounds (e.g., $d(\varepsilon_3)^{49}<.142^{120}$ at $j=2$, with subsequent steps verified by squaring), with $\varepsilon_5<.000015$.


Induction on scale verifies Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-x} for all subsequent scales: the base case $j=5$ is given by the table above.
Assuming the bound at scale $j$ with $x=x_j\le.01$, the relation $k_j=M_j/2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 q_j\le\sqrt{\frac{6x^2}{2-18x^2}}\le2x,\\
 F(2x,x,1-x)\le\frac{6x^2}{(1-2x)(1-x)^3}<7x^2.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:b-x-e}
\end{equation}
Setting $E_{j+1}=10x^2$ satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-E-update}.
The four-factor product in Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-eta-update} is at least $1-2x-16x^2$, and $f(2x+16x^2)<3x^2$, so $\eta_{j+1}=10x^2$ satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-eta-update} with $\varepsilon_{j+1}\le6x^2$, completing the induction for Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-x}.
