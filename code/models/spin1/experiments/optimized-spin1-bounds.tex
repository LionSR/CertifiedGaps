\section{Comparison with the numerical bulk gap}
For the same convention $J=1$, the large-system numerical estimates are
\[
 \Delta_{S=1}\simeq0.41048(6),\qquad
 \Delta_{S=2}\simeq0.08917(4).
\]
These are the estimates in Table I of S.~Todo and K.~Kato,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9911047}{\emph{Cluster Algorithms for
General-$S$ Quantum Spin Systems}}, Phys. Rev. Lett. \textbf{87}, 047203 (2001).
That table reports spatial correlation lengths $6.0153(3)$ and $49.49(1)$,
respectively. The quoted numerical uncertainties are not rigorous spectral
enclosures. The spin-one DMRG estimate $0.41050(2)$ of White and Huse,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.48.3844}{Phys. Rev. B \textbf{48}, 3844
(1993)}, is consistent with this scale.

The temperature-lifting lower bound is approximately $17.76\%$ of the
numerical bulk gap, compared with $4.14\%$ for our earlier $0.016978269$
bound and $0.93\%$ for the published $\log(20)/784$ bound.
It improves our earlier bound by about $4.29$ and the published bound by
about $19.1$, while remaining about $5.63$ times below the numerical value.
These statements compare
a uniform bound on finite periodic chains with the numerical bulk benchmark;
we have not independently reproduced the quoted thermodynamic-limit gap.

The selected quantity $A_j=(1-p_j)^2(1-q_j)$ below is a dimensionless
\emph{purity lower bound}, not a gap estimate. Along the successful bootstrap,
where both length and inverse temperature grow as $2^j$, the controlled
defects tend to zero and $A_j\to1$. The gap information is in the rate,
through $\log[A_j/(1-A_j)]/b_j$, rather than the limiting value $1$ itself.
This is not a statement that purity tends to one at fixed temperature as
length grows.

\section{Cover the early lengths before applying the uniform theorem}
The following finite-prefix version is a direct consequence of the paper's
coupled update, purity interpolation, and uniform bootstrap proposition.

\begin{proposition}
Suppose $p_j,q_j$ are valid upper bounds on the spatial and temporal defects
at $(n_j,b_j)=(2^jn_0,2^jb_0)$ for $0\le j\le K$. Put
$A_j=(1-p_j)^2(1-q_j)$ and suppose $A_j>1/2$ for $j<K$.
Suppose $u=\max(p_K,q_K)$ and $C$ satisfy
\[
 0<u<1/6,\quad
 G(u):=\tfrac12\big((1-u)^{-1}+(1-u)^{-2}+(1-u)^{-3}\big)^2\le C,
 \quad Cu<1,\quad C(1-3u)>3.
\]
If $g$ is strictly smaller than every member of
\[
 \left\{\frac{\log(A_j/(1-A_j))}{b_j}:0\le j<K\right\}
 \cup\left\{\frac{\log(1/(Cu))}{b_K}\right\},
\]
then the ground state is unique and $\gamma_L>g$ for every even $L\ge n_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $n_j\le L\le2n_j$, the interpolation inequality gives
$T(L,b_j)\ge A_j^{L/(2n_j)}\ge A_j>1/2$. The largest physical Gibbs weight
$w_0$ is at least this purity, so it is unique. For a weight $w_1$ at the
first excited energy,
\[
 e^{-b_j\gamma_L}=w_1/w_0\le(1-w_0)/w_0\le(1-A_j)/A_j.
\]
This proves the required estimate on each finite interval. The original
uniform bootstrap proposition covers every even $L\ge n_K$.
The intervals $[n_j,2n_j]$ for $j<K$ and the last half-line cover all
even lengths at least $n_0$.
\end{proof}

Use the original update $f(u)=u^2/[2(1-u)^2]$, retaining the two defects:
\begin{align*}

 q_{j+1}&=\left\lceil f(1-(1-q_j)^2(1-p_{j+1}))\right\rceil_{100},
 \qquad\lceil x\rceil_{100}=10^{-100}\lceil10^{100}x\rceil.
\end{align*}

\section{Temperature lifting before the purity bootstrap}
\label{sec:temperature-lifting}

The following recombination of the same finite inputs gives a larger gain.
It generalizes the paper's source-2 temperature argument to the stronger
source-1 moments, retaining the threefold sector multiplicity at every
required moment order.

Use the tighter rational $U,V,m_J,m_N$ above and put $b_0=49/4$.
For every even $k\ge12$, capped masses give
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal J(k)&=U^k+(m_J-U^{12})^{k/12},\\
 \mathcal N(k)&=V^k+(m_N-V^{12})^{k/12},\\
 Z_k(b_0)&\le M_k:=\mathcal J(k)+3\mathcal N(k),\qquad
 Z_k(b_0,P)\le\mathcal J(k).
\end{align*}
Indeed $U^{12}<m_J<2U^{12}$ and $V^{12}<m_N<2V^{12}$.
The last inequality uses $Z_k(b_0,P)=J_k-N_k$ and $N_k\ge0$.

Choose $r\ge1$ and an even $12\le k\le60$. Because each twisted partition
function is itself the Gibbs trace of a self-adjoint Hamiltonian, raising
its positive Boltzmann factors to the power $r$ gives
\[
 Z_k(rb_0)\le D:=M_k^r,\qquad
 Z_k(rb_0,P)\le E:=\mathcal J(k)^r.
\]
Outward rational upper bounds may replace $D,E$. In the new spatial
operator at $b=rb_0$, set $w_i=|\lambda_i(b)|^k$ and
\[
 h=60/k\ge1,\qquad \tau=(D+3E)/4.
\]
Then $\sum_iw_i\le D$, the $J$-sector mass is at most $\tau$, and
the existing length-120 trial gives
\[
 \sum_iw_i^{2h}=Z_{120}(b)>1.
\]

Choose a rational $t>0$ with $Dt^{2h-1}<1$ and $3t>D$.
Some weight $w_0>t$ exists, since otherwise the last sum is at most
$Dt^{2h-1}<1$. This weight belongs to $J$: every $N$ weight occurs
three times, which would contradict the total mass bound.
After removing $w_0$, let $x$ be the remaining $J$ mass. Then
$0\le x\le\tau-t$, while the full remaining $N$ mass is at most
$D-t-x$. For every $s\ge1$, the residual $s$th moment is at most
\[
 R_s(t)=\max\left\{
   3\left(\frac{D-t}{3}\right)^s,
   (\tau-t)^s+3\left(\frac{D-\tau}{3}\right)^s
 \right\}.
\]
To prove this, bound each sector list by the $s$th power of its total mass.
The three identical $N$ lists contribute at most
$3[(D-t-x)/3]^s$. The resulting convex function of $x$ attains its maximum
at one of the two endpoints. All quantities are nonnegative because
$t\le\tau\le D$.

We may repeatedly replace $t$ by a larger rational $t'$ whenever
\[
 (t')^{2h}+R_{2h}(t)<1.
\]
If $w_0\le t'$, this inequality would contradict $Z_{120}(b)>1$.
Using $R_{2h}$ directly preserves more multiplicity information than
squaring an upper bound on $R_h$.
Consequently a spatial seed at length 60 is
\[
 S(60,b)\ge\left(1+\frac{R_h(t)}{t^h}\right)^{-2}.
\]

The temporal seed uses the same trial in the physical spectrum.
Write $Q=M_{120}>1$. At $b_0$, the ground Boltzmann factor $z_0>1$
and the total mass is at most $Q$. Hence
\[
 \sum_{i>0}\frac{z_i}{z_0}\le Q-1,\qquad
 \sum_{i>0}\left(\frac{z_i}{z_0}\right)^r\le(Q-1)^r.
\]
This yields the second seed without a new contraction:
\[
 T(120,b)\ge\bigl(1+(Q-1)^r\bigr)^{-2}.
\]
The spatial weights $w_i$ and physical Boltzmann factors $z_i$ play
different roles; the proof uses the original transfer theorem to connect
their partition functions.

\subsection{A conditional criterion using one thermal upper bound}
The improved trial permits a useful simplification of the thermal task.
Write $\widehat Z_L(b)=\Tr e^{-b(H_L+aL)}$; scalar shifts preserve the purities
and rescale the self-adjoint spatial transfer operator.

\begin{proposition}[One-trace temperature lifting]
Let $n\ge4$ be even, let $h,r\ge1$ be integers, and suppose
\[
 \widehat Z_n(\beta)\le U,\qquad E_0(2hn)<-2hna,\qquad U>1.
\]
Set $D=U^r$ and $Q=U^{2h}$. Choose $t_0>0$ satisfying
$Dt_0^{2h-1}<1$ and $2t_0>D$. One may take $t=t_0$, or any
$t\in[t_0,1]$ satisfying $t^{2h}+(D-t)^{2h}<1$. Then
\[
 S(hn,r\beta)\ge
 \left[1+\left(\frac{D-t}{t}\right)^h\right]^{-2},\qquad
 T(2hn,r\beta)\ge[1+(Q-1)^r]^{-2}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The physical power inequality gives $\widehat Z_n(r\beta)\le U^r=D$.
For the shifted spatial transfer eigenvalues at $r\beta$, put
$w_i=|\lambda_i|^n$. Thus $\sum_iw_i\le D$ and
$\sum_iw_i^{2h}=\widehat Z_{2hn}(r\beta)>1$ by the variational hypothesis.
If $\max_iw_i\le t_0$, then the latter sum is at most
$Dt_0^{2h-1}<1$, a contradiction. The largest weight therefore exceeds
$t_0>D/2$. On this interval, $x^{2h}+(D-x)^{2h}$ is increasing.
Bounding the remaining $2h$-moment by $(D-x)^{2h}$ proves that the largest
weight also exceeds any admissible $t$. The remaining $h$-moment is at most
$(D-t)^h$, proving the spatial estimate.

At inverse temperature $\beta$, the spatial moment inequality gives
$\widehat Z_{2hn}(\beta)\le U^{2h}=Q$. Its ground-state Boltzmann weight
exceeds one, so the sum of all excited weights relative to the ground
weight is less than $Q-1$. Raising inverse temperature by the integer $r$
bounds this sum by $(Q-1)^r$. The squared ground-state probability alone
gives the stated thermal-purity bound.
\end{proof}

For example, with $n=128$, $\beta=32$, $h=2$, $r=3$, and $U=1.08$, exact
rational scalar checks give the \emph{conditional} conclusion
\[

\]
The length-512 variational hypothesis is supplied by the certificate above
with $a=4.76122$. However, at this shift the length-128 trial already gives
\[

 e^{32\cdot128(4.761268026385-4.76122)}>1.1967>1.08.
\]
Thus this illustrative thermal target is \textbf{ruled out}, rather than
merely unverified. Larger thermal lengths and more accurate trial shifts
must be explored. The proposition supplies a family of conditional tests,
not a spin-two gap proof.
The script \texttt{spin2/scripts/one\_trace\_target.jl} and its 63 tests
check the conditional scalar argument.

\subsection{Thermal upper bounds and compression}
Split the even ring into disjoint odd and even bond layers, $H=A+B$.
The Araki--Lieb--Thirring inequality and the product-formula limit imply
\[
 Z_N(\beta)\le
 \Tr\left(e^{-\beta A/m}e^{-\beta B/m}\right)^m,
 \qquad m\in\mathbb N.
\]
Thus a certified contraction of the right side needs no separate estimate
of the Trotter error. A Julia reference implementation uses exact rational
two-site spectral projectors and outward-rounded Arb products in every
magnetization sector. Its 42 checks include the independently known
four-site spectrum. It currently treats only reference sizes.

After the Marshall transformation the gates are nonnegative entrywise.
A candidate compression can therefore be replaced by rational nonnegative
factors satisfying $M\le UV$ entrywise, checked exactly; subsequent positive
contractions preserve this inequality. A four-site prototype bounds the
thermal amplitude in this way. At $\beta=4$, its upper bound exceeds the
uncompressed Trotter trace by a factor about $1.00844$ at bond dimension 569
(the full cut has dimension 625). This establishes a small-system compression
certificate, but not yet an efficient scheme for the large thermal traces
needed above.

Repeated compression is more demanding than this single-compression test.
Starting at $\beta=1/8$ and making five squarings, with compression at the
initial stage and after each squaring, gives the following four-site results
at $\beta=4$, with 64 Trotter steps. Each entry is the certified upper trace
divided by the uncompressed Trotter trace; displayed ratios are approximate.

For the second row, let $A$ be the exact Hermitian thermal amplitude and
$B$ its Hermitian approximation. If
$\|A-B\|_{\mathrm F}\le\varepsilon$ and $\|B\|\le b$, and a new
approximation $C$ satisfies $\|B^2-C\|_{\mathrm F}\le\delta$, then
\[
 \|A^2-C\|_{\mathrm F}\le
 \delta+\varepsilon(2b+\varepsilon).
\]
Indeed, writing $E=A-B$ gives
$A^2-B^2=BE+EB+E^2$ and applying the operator/Hilbert--Schmidt norm

\[
 \Tr A^2\le(\|B\|_{\mathrm F}+\varepsilon)^2.
\]
The verifier includes outward-rounded matrix-product errors in $\delta$.
SVD truncation respects charge and transpose parity; rational rounding
preserves Hermiticity without doubling the represented rank.

The third row uses a different induction. Suppose $M\ge0$ and its ordered
eigenvalues dominate those of $A\ge0$. Given Hermitian $C$ and
$\|M^2-C\|\le\delta$, put $M'=C+\delta I$. Then
$M'\ge M^2\ge0$. The min--max principle gives
\[
 \lambda_i(M')\ge\lambda_i(M^2)=\lambda_i(M)^2
 \ge\lambda_i(A)^2=\lambda_i(A^2).
\]
This preserves spectral domination at every squaring. The final
$\Tr[(M')^2]$ therefore bounds the desired trace. Adding the identity costs
at most one additional Schmidt term.

Both norm certificates currently obtain an operator-norm bound by enumerating
the four-site magnetization sectors. In the rational polynomial gauge,

 \|R\|_{\rm physical}\le
 \max_i\frac{(|R|v)_i}{v_i},

\subsection{A local MPO norm witness and its present limitation}
For an MPO core $W^{[j]}_{b,s,t,a}$ in an orthonormal physical basis, let
$L_b$ denote the partial operators to its left, with $\|L_b\|\le l_b$.
The next partial operator has matrix entries
$X^a_{st}=\sum_bW^{[j]}_{b,s,t,a}L_b$. Hence
\[
 C^a_{st}=\sum_b|W^{[j]}_{b,s,t,a}|l_b,
 \qquad
 \|X^a\|\le\|C^a\|_2
 \le\sqrt{\|C^a\|_1\|C^a\|_\infty}.
\]
The first inequality follows by bounding the norms of the physical block
components of an input vector. Applying this recursively, also from the
right, supplies upper bounds at every MPO cut. The calculation uses only
local tensors. Rational square-root upper bounds are accepted by integer
squaring comparisons.

The Julia implementation \texttt{Spin2MPONorms.jl} passes 33 checks,
including an exact norm-one result for a 128-site identity MPO and dense
controls on random small MPOs. It currently loses too much cancellation:
on the four-site ITensors candidates at $\beta=1$, the resulting bounds
exceed 25 although their numerical Hilbert--Schmidt norm is one.
These bounds concern the exact quantized candidate tensors; their error
relative to the physical thermal operator has not been certified.
A tighter norm construction is required before this route can provide
the missing thermal input.

\subsection{Gauge optimization and certified MPO squaring}
A sharper norm witness regards a local core as a matrix from
$(b,t)$ to $(a,s)$. Sequential composition bounds the full MPO norm by
the product of these local matrix norms. Positive rational diagonal
changes of virtual basis cancel exactly on internal bonds. Numerical
optimization proposes these gauges and local norm bounds $\alpha_j$;
outward-rounded Arb Cholesky accepts a bound only when
$\alpha_j^2 I-W_j^*W_j$ (or the smaller reversed Gram matrix) is
certainly positive definite.

For the quantum-number-aware ITensors candidates at $\beta=1/8$, the
four-site product decreases from $3.11769$ to a certified bound below
$0.16689992$, and the eight-site product from $38.97081$ to below
$0.04945418$. Their numerical Hilbert--Schmidt norms are one.
At $\beta=1$, the respective certified products remain above one,
so the method still loses too much information there. Saved exact
rational tensors, gauges, and local bounds permit independent replay
without trusting ITensors or a numerical eigensolver. The norm tests
now comprise 68 passing checks.

These local bounds also give a compression certificate without forming
a four-layer transfer matrix. Let $R_a$ be the right-tail operators of
an MPO, and suppose the coefficient map
$v\mapsto\sum_a v_a R_a$ has norm at most $\gamma$ from Euclidean
space to Hilbert--Schmidt space. In a separately gauged copy put
$A_a=g_aR_a$, and suppose $\|[A_1\ \cdots\ A_D]\|\le b$. Then
\[
 \left\|\sum_{a,b}V_{ab}A_aR_b\right\|_{\mathrm F}
 \le b\gamma\|V\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
Indeed, factor this operator into the block row of the $A_a$ and the
column of operators $\sum_bV_{ab}R_b$. The latter column has squared
Hilbert--Schmidt norm at most $\gamma^2\sum_{a,b}|V_{ab}|^2$.
Both $b$ and $\gamma$ have local interval norm witnesses.
The additional cap $b\le\gamma(\sum_a g_a^2)^{1/2}$ and optional
two-site blocks can sharpen them.

In a left-to-right compression, write the exact local identities as
$R_{j-1}P_j=Q_jR_j+E_j$, where $P_j$ is the squared input core and
the final step has zero residual. If the preceding $Q$-prefix map has
norm at most $c_{j-1}$ and $\|E_j\|_{\mathrm F}\le\eta_j$, telescoping
gives
\[
 \|B^2-C\|_{\mathrm F}
 \le \sum_j c_{j-1}\eta_j b_{j+1}\gamma_{j+1}.
\]
The Julia verifier checks the residuals rationally and all local norm
bounds by interval Cholesky. It does not assume exact numerical
canonical form or Hermiticity of the approximate MPO. Its 59 tests
include independent dense contractions and rejection of corrupted witnesses.

The completed bond-dimension-eight tests at initial $\beta=1/128$
give relative squaring-error upper bounds $0.020357$, $0.053945$, and
$0.134228$ for 8, 16, and 32 sites, respectively. All saved rational
witnesses pass independent replay. The denominator here is the output
MPO's Hilbert--Schmidt norm. These errors are still too large for the
needed repeated squaring, and the initial MPO's distance from the exact
Gibbs amplitude remains uncertified. Consequently these advances do
not yet establish a thermal seed or a uniform spin-two gap.

Reducing the starting step and allowing the output rank to exceed the
input rank is more effective. For the same exact sixteen-site input
at $\beta=1/1024$ and input bond dimension 16, the relative

\text{error upper bound}&0.00542631&0.000693457&1.0764\,10^{-5}&1.431\,10^{-6}&4.81\,10^{-13}.
\end{array}
\]
The refinements reuse saved rational input tensors and gauges and raise

\subsection{An exact polynomial starting object}
There is a second thermal upper-bound route that avoids a product formula.
Put $K=H_N+NI$. The two-site spin-two eigenvalues
$-6,-5,-3,0,4$ imply $\|K\|\le5N$. For $m=2^k$ and
$\delta=\beta/(2m)$, choose rational
\[
 r\ge e^{5N\delta}\frac{(5N\delta)^3}{6},\qquad
 Q=I-\delta K+\tfrac12\delta^2K^2+rI.
\]
The scalar Taylor remainder and functional calculus give
$Q\ge e^{-\delta K}>0$. Because both are functions of $K$, their
eigenvalues can be raised to the power $2m$, giving
\[
 Z_N(\beta)\le e^{\beta N}\Tr Q^{2m}
 =e^{\beta N}\|Q^m\|_{\mathrm F}^2.
\]
This uses commutation, not a general operator-monotonicity assertion.
Furthermore $0\le Q-e^{-\delta K}\le2rI$ and
$e^{-\delta K}\ge e^{-5N\delta}I$. Hence the exact polynomial upper
trace is at most
\[
 \exp\!\left(4mr e^{5N\delta}\right) Z_N(\beta).
\]
At $N=256$, $\beta=64$, and $k=28$, outward-rounded scalar arithmetic
bounds this factor by $1.000636180$. Thus this starting approximation
costs less than $0.064\%$, before any MPO compression. The shifted
target would require a certified upper bound on
\[
 e^{-(a-1)\beta N}\|Q^m\|_{\mathrm F}^2.
\]
The code \texttt{Spin2PolynomialBounds.jl} passes 263 checks, including
comparison with the independent exact four-site spectrum. The scalar
majorant is established; a rigorous long-chain MPO contraction of its
powers is still missing. No spin-two gap follows from the scalar
approximation estimate alone.

\subsection{Exact compression and the remaining norm bottleneck}
The rational polynomial representation and its physical metric are now
implemented. In the basis with weights $w=(1,4,6,4,1)$, a coefficient
$X_{st}$ has squared Hilbert--Schmidt weight $w_t/w_s$. Local norm
certificates use rational weighted Gram matrices and outward-rounded
Cholesky checks. Bounds are rounded at a relative scale, including for
the exponentially small norms of normalized operators.

The initial construction represents $K$ with bond dimension $8$ and
$Q$ with bond dimension $73$. Exact rational row reduction removes
dependencies from both ends. For $N=16$ and $N=256$ the resulting maximum
bond dimension is $34$; for $N=4$ it is $33$. This introduces
\emph{zero} compression error. The saved witnesses contain local
intertwining matrices $T_j$ satisfying
\[
 W_j T_{j+1}=T_j\widetilde W_j,\qquad T_1=T_{N+1}=1.
\]
These identities telescope to equality of the full operators and have
passed independent exact replay at all three lengths. This replaces the
earlier dimension-$16$ starting approximation, whose certified relative
error at $N=256$ was $1.12\times10^{-12}$, when an exact starting object
is preferable.

The first complete short flow uses approximate Gram products
$B_j^*B_j$ and an exact spatial reflection after each compression.
Reflection preserves operator and Hilbert--Schmidt norms and permits
alternating the compression direction without another rounding step.
At $N=16$, four dimension-$16$ steps, starting from the earlier rounded
seed, pass independent replay. They reach only
$\beta=3.814697265625\times10^{-6}$. Their forward normalized
Hilbert--Schmidt error bound is $9.95\times10^{-8}$; backward Gram norm
bounds reduce it to $8.94\times10^{-8}$. This is not a useful
low-temperature certificate yet.

The exact seed also supplies the rational spectral bound
\[
 a_0=\frac{1+r+5N\delta+(5N\delta)^2/2}{5^{N/2}}
 \ge\|Q/5^{N/2}\|.
\]
Under the recorded positive normalizations $\kappa_j$, reference
norm bounds obey $a_{j+1}\ge a_j^2/\kappa_j$. Expanding a Gram-product
error around either the exact reference or its approximation permits
using the smaller of their separately certified norm bounds in
$\epsilon_{j+1}\le[\delta_j+\epsilon_j(2b_j+\epsilon_j)]/\kappa_j$.
For the same four-step run, this reduces the final error further to
$4.180\times10^{-9}$, about $24$ times smaller than the original
forward bound. The entire source flow was independently replayed
before applying this scalar refinement.

An operator-error verifier now bounds the same local compression
residuals with independent rational gauges on the output prefix and
both input tails. If $p_{j-1}$ bounds the prefix column operator,
$e_j$ the gauged residual, and $t^{(1)}_{j+1},t^{(2)}_{j+1}$ the two
tail row operators, telescoping gives
\[
 \|B^*B-C\|\le
 \sum_j p_{j-1}e_jt^{(1)}_{j+1}t^{(2)}_{j+1}.
\]
For the first saved sixteen-site step this gives
$2.673\times10^{-18}$, whereas the earlier Hilbert--Schmidt estimate
was $8.728\times10^{-17}$. These bounds control different norms;
the operator estimate is intended for the high-entropy part of the
flow. It does not by itself bound the final thermal trace.

A checked scalar bridge can connect such an operator estimate to a
later trace-moment certificate. If $A\ge0$ acts on a space of dimension
$d$, $\|A-B\|\le\eta$, and $\Tr|B|^p\le M$ for even $p\ge2$, then
for every $t>0$,
\[
 \Tr A^p\le(1+t)^{p-1}M
       +d(1+1/t)^{p-1}\eta^p.
\]
This follows from singular-value perturbation and scalar convexity.
For example, if $d=5^{256}$, $p=64$, $M\le1$, and
$\eta\le10^{-8}$, choosing $t=625\eta$ gives an upper factor below
$1.0005$. The required long-chain operator-error and trace-moment
inputs have not yet been certified. The remaining task is still the
full low-temperature trace bound, and no uniform spin-two gap has
been established.

\subsection{A trace correction that retains cancellations}
The preceding norm bounds can be wasteful when the desired observable is
only the final squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm. Let $A$ be the operator before
one compression, let $C$ be its compressed approximation, and suppose
$\|A-C\|_{\mathrm F}\le\eta$. The exact identity
\[
 \|A\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =2\operatorname{Re}\Tr(C^*A)-\|C\|_{\mathrm F}^2
   +\|A-C\|_{\mathrm F}^2
\]
gives the upper certificate
\[
 \|A\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \le 2\operatorname{Re}\Tr(C^*A)-\|C\|_{\mathrm F}^2+\eta^2.
\]
An upper enclosure of the signed overlap and a lower enclosure of
$\|C\|_{\mathrm F}^2$ suffice. The remaining residual contribution is
quadratic, whereas the triangle certificate
$(\|C\|_{\mathrm F}+\eta)^2$ has a linear error term.
For a squaring $A=B^2$ (or a Gram product $B^*B$), the overlap has three
MPO layers, rather than the four layers of the direct squared norm.
This changes the contraction cost and the certificate simultaneously.

The script \texttt{trace\_correction\_probe.jl} replays the rational local
residual certificates and encloses the overlaps with ball arithmetic.

\subsection{A backward certificate for the complete trace flow}
Use the real Hilbert--Schmidt pairing
$\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{Re}\Tr(X^*Y)$.
Let $S_j$ be either the identity or spatial reflection, and set
\[
 A_{j+1}=S_j(A_j^*A_j)/\kappa_j,\qquad
 D_j=A_j-B_j,\qquad
 R_j=S_j(B_j^*B_j)/\kappa_j-B_{j+1},
\]
where $\kappa_j>0$. No Hermiticity of the approximations $B_j$ is
required. Choose backward approximations $G_j$ with $G_K=B_K$, and define
\[
 T_j=\frac{B_j\bigl(S_j(G_{j+1})+S_j(G_{j+1})^*\bigr)}{\kappa_j}-G_j.
\]
Suppose separately verified bounds give
$\|D_j\|_{\mathrm F}\le\epsilon_j$,
$\|T_j\|_{\mathrm F}\le\tau_j$, and
$\|G_{j+1}\|\le\gamma_j$. Define
\begin{align*}
 c&=\|B_K\|_{\mathrm F}^2+2\langle G_0,A_0-B_0\rangle
       +2\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\langle G_{j+1},R_j\rangle,\\
 r&=2\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}
       \left(\tau_j\epsilon_j+\frac{\gamma_j\epsilon_j^2}{\kappa_j}\right).
\end{align*}
Then
\[
 \boxed{c-r\ \le\ \|A_K\|_{\mathrm F}^2
          \ \le\ c+r+\epsilon_K^2.}
\]
For interval contractions, replace $c$ by its lower or upper endpoint
on the corresponding side.

Indeed, expanding the Gram step gives
\[
 D_{j+1}=R_j+
 \frac{S_j(B_j^*D_j+D_j^*B_j+D_j^*D_j)}{\kappa_j}.
\]
Pairing with $G_{j+1}$ transfers the linear part to $G_j+T_j$.
Telescoping and expanding $\|B_K+D_K\|_{\mathrm F}^2$ yield
\[
 \|A_K\|_{\mathrm F}^2=c+\|D_K\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 +2\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\left(
 \langle T_j,D_j\rangle+
 \frac{\langle G_{j+1},S_j(D_j^*D_j)\rangle}{\kappa_j}\right).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the first remainder; operator/trace-norm duality
bounds the second by $\gamma_j\epsilon_j^2/\kappa_j$.
Thus the signed linear errors are retained, and the remaining errors
are products of primal and backward errors or squares of primal errors.

The backward operator norms need not be replaced by Hilbert--Schmidt
norms. If $b_j\ge\|B_j\|$ and $g_K\ge\|B_K\|$, the same dual defects give
\[
 g_j=\frac{2b_jg_{j+1}}{\kappa_j}+\tau_j\ \ge\ \|G_j\|.
\]
At each step one may take the smaller of this bound and a separately
certified Hilbert--Schmidt norm. This avoids an unnecessary
dimension-dependent loss when the thermal operator is close to a
multiple of the identity.

\subsection{Certifying the benefit of local orthogonality}
The sum of local truncation errors can be replaced by a backward
quadratic recurrence that checks the cross terms. At cut $j$, write
the local compression as $M_j=U_jR_j+E_j$, and let $W_j$ be its physical
row metric. In addition to the existing bounds
$\|E_j\|_{\mathrm F,W_j}\le\delta_j$ and
$\|U_j\|\le\alpha_j$, certify
\[
 \|U_j^{\mathsf T}W_jE_j\|_{\mathrm F}\le\theta_j.
\]
Let $\Gamma_{j+1}$ bound the remaining product-tail coefficient map.
If $e_{j+1}$ bounds the suffix compression error, then
\[
 \|D_j\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le
 (\delta_j\Gamma_{j+1})^2+\alpha_j^2e_{j+1}^2
 +2\theta_j\Gamma_{j+1}e_{j+1},\qquad e_N=0.
\]
The quantity $D_j$ is the actual suffix error; $e_j$ is chosen as
an outward-rounded upper square root of the right-hand side. To prove the estimate, decompose
the suffix error as $E_jT_{j+1}+U_jD_{j+1}$ and move $U_j$ across the
weighted inner product in the cross term. The two factors are bounded
by $\theta_j\Gamma_{j+1}$ and $e_{j+1}$.

For exact orthogonal projections $\theta_j=0$, recovering the usual
sum-of-squares benefit. Rounded singular vectors need not be orthogonal:
the stored $\theta_j$ bounds their actual defects, checked directly in
rational or outward-rounded interval arithmetic. One may also use
$\theta_j\le\alpha_j\delta_j$ and retain the previous triangle bound
whenever it is smaller after rounding. The implementation can keep all
saved cores and bridges fixed while recomputing this sharper certificate.
Small-system tests compare against the exact physical operator and reject
a corrupted projection-defect bound.

\section{Higher-order initialization by positive polynomial squares}

The number of subsequent squarings can be reduced by increasing the degree
of the initial polynomial. Write $K=H+NI$, $M=5N$, $m=2^p$, and
$\delta=\beta/(2m)$. For any positive integer $d$, choose a rational
\[
 r\ge e^{\delta M}\frac{(\delta M)^{d+1}}{(d+1)!},\qquad
 Q=\sum_{j=0}^d\frac{(-\delta K)^j}{j!}+rI.
\]
Taylor's remainder and functional calculus give
\[
 e^{-\delta K}\le Q\le e^{-\delta K}+2rI
 \le(1+2r e^{\delta M})e^{-\delta K}.
\]
These operators commute, so
\[
 Z_H(\beta)\le e^{\beta N}\operatorname{Tr}Q^{2m}
 \le \exp(4mr e^{\delta M})Z_H(\beta).
\]
The following scalar bounds at $N=256$, $\beta=64$ have been checked with
outward-rounded arithmetic; displayed upper bounds are rounded upward.

Degree $d$ & Subsequent squarings $p$ & Inflation upper bound\\

For the fourth-order polynomial there is an exact rational identity
\[
 P_4(x)=\left(1+\frac{x}{2}+\frac{x^2}{12}\right)^2
       +\frac1{12}\left(x+\frac{x^2}{2}\right)^2
       +\frac1{72}(x^2)^2.
\]
It permits construction from three quadratic Gram certificates. This is
systematic: for $P_{2k}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2k}x^j/j!$,
\[
 P_{2k}(x)=\frac1{(2k)!}\int_0^\infty e^{-t}(t+x)^{2k}\,dt.
\]
The moment Gram matrix of $(t+x)^k$ is rational and positive definite.
An exact $LDL^{\mathsf T}$ decomposition gives positive rational weights
times squares of degree-$k$ polynomials. The implementation checks the
coefficient identity exactly, including examples through degree $32$.

To track normalization, put $\sigma=5^{-N/2}$ and
$A_i=\sigma p_i(-\delta K)$ for the three quadratic factors above.
They are self-adjoint, and
\[
 \sigma Q=\sigma^{-1}\sum_i w_i A_i^*A_i+r\sigma I.
\]
If $\|C_i-A_i^*A_i\|_{\rm HS}\le\eta_i$ and a final compressed
candidate $B$ differs from
$\sigma^{-1}\sum_iw_i C_i+r\sigma I$ by at most $\rho$, then
\[
 \|B-\sigma Q\|_{\rm HS}\le
 \rho+\sigma^{-1}\sum_iw_i\eta_i.
\]

a separate contraction for the final compression error. This trades
three initial Gram constructions for seven fewer subsequent squarings.
A net runtime improvement and a 256-site fourth-order seed certificate
have not yet been established.

\paragraph{Lossless witness encoding.}
Large dyadic denominators can be stored by their binary exponents, without
altering any coefficient. On the existing three-step four-site fixture,
this reduces the combined witness size from $7{,}562{,}803$ to
$5{,}768{,}539$ bytes, or $23.7\%$. Every decoded field agrees exactly
with its predecessor, and the reencoded flow passes independent replay.
Legacy rational encodings remain readable. This is a storage improvement;
an end-to-end runtime improvement has not been measured.

\paragraph{Separate gauges for separate inequalities.}
The complete-operator norm and the compression residual need not use the
same auxiliary gauges. If a second family $F_j$, with identity boundary
gauges, gives independently verified local operator bounds $v_j$, then
$b_F=\prod_jv_j$ bounds the complete input operator. Hence the Gram error
coefficient may use $b=\min\{b_F,b_G,b_{\rm HS}\}$, while retaining the
original residual-tail bounds from the family $G_j$. In particular, the
global bound $b_F$ is never substituted for a suffix-map bound.
The verifier supports this additional certificate and checks its gauge
cancellations and local inequalities. Explicit small-operator tests pass
in exact and interval arithmetic and reject corrupted auxiliary bounds.
The four-step integration run reaches $\beta=1/2048$ with an independently
replayed Hilbert--Schmidt error bound of $1.311\times10^{-9}$.
An additional $256$-site improvement from this separation has not
yet been demonstrated.

\paragraph{Full matrix gauges for operator errors.}

and $P_j$ the new output-prefix gauge. The exact residual transforms by
\[
 E'_{q'sta'b'}=\sum_{q,a,b}(P_j)_{q'q}E_{qstab}
 (G_{j+1}F_{j+1}^{-1})_{aa'}(S_{j+1}^{-1})_{bb'}.
\]
The prefix and tail gauge factors cancel in the full telescoping identity.
Three separate auxiliary-axis contractions implement this formula without
forming a Kronecker matrix. The checker visits one residual at a time;
interval arithmetic has an exact local fallback. All $138$ tests pass,
including exact reconstruction of the complete residual on small systems

\paragraph{Backward Schatten propagation.}
A systematic alternative to forward error propagation uses a different
Schatten norm at each doubling. Let $D=5^N$, let $k$ be the number of
Gram compressions, and put $p_j=2^{k+1-j}$. Suppose $C_j$ approximates
$B_{j-1}^\dagger B_{j-1}$ with certified Hilbert--Schmidt and operator
errors $\zeta_j$ and $\sigma_j$, respectively, and $B_j$ equals
$C_j/\kappa_j$, up to a unitary spatial reflection, with $\kappa_j>0$.
Starting from $m_k\geq\lVert B_k\rVert_2$, define, with upward rounding,
\[
 m_{j-1}=\sqrt{\kappa_jm_j+
       \min\{\zeta_j,D^{1/p_j}\sigma_j\}}.
\]
The Schatten triangle inequality and
$\lVert B^\dagger B\rVert_p=\lVert B\rVert_{2p}^2$ prove inductively that
$m_j\geq\lVert B_j\rVert_{p_j}$. An unavailable operator certificate
is omitted from the minimum. If $A_0=Q/\sqrt D$ is the exact positive
polynomial seed and $A_0-B_0$ has certified errors $\eta_2,\eta_\infty$,
then
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}Q^{p_0}
 \leq D^{p_0/2}
 \bigl(m_0+\min\{\eta_2,D^{1/p_0}\eta_\infty\}\bigr)^{p_0}.
\]
For $Q\geq e^{-\delta(H+NI)}$, the two operators commute, so at
$\beta=\delta p_0$ this gives a shifted thermal trace upper bound after
multiplication by $e^{-(a-1)N\beta}$.

This recurrence avoids the forward global-operator amplification factors.
At early steps $D^{1/p_j}$ is close to one; at late steps the minimum
can use the Hilbert--Schmidt error. The implementation checks every
rounded square root by an exact rational squaring inequality and obtains
dimension factors by repeated square roots. All $107$ additional tests
pass, including explicit matrix chains, non-Hermitian candidates,
reflections, and the exponent required for $28$ squarings at $N=256$.
A replay script connects these scalar inequalities to saved MPO witnesses.
The required low-temperature long-chain inputs remain unproved; this
recurrence is not itself a spin-$2$ gap certificate.
