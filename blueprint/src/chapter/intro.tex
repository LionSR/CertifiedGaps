\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

Proving that a quantum spin chain is gapped in the thermodynamic limit requires a lower bound on
the energy difference between the first excited state and the ground state that is uniform in the
system size. The \emph{spacetime bootstrap}, also called the \emph{two-purity doubling criterion},
reduces this infinite-volume problem on periodic rings to two finite-size inequalities~\cite{OpenAIHaldane}.
This document develops the complete mathematical proof of the criterion and its applications to
concrete quantum spin chains and ladders.

\section{The spacetime-bootstrap criterion}

For a periodic ring Hamiltonian \(H_L = \sum_{j=1}^{L} h_{j,j+1} + \sum_{j=1}^{L} o_j\) of \(L\)
unit cells, write \(Z_L(\beta) = \Tr e^{-\beta H_L}\) for the partition function
(Definition~\ref{def:partition-function}) and define the \emph{spatial purity} and \emph{thermal
purity} (Definition~\ref{def:purities}) by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:intro-purities}
  \Ps(n,\beta) = \frac{Z_{2n}(\beta)}{Z_n(\beta)^2}, \qquad
  \Pt(n,\beta) = \frac{Z_n(2\beta)}{Z_n(\beta)^2}.
\end{equation}
A thermal purity \(\Pt(L, \beta) > 1/2\) means that the ground state carries more than half of the
Boltzmann weight. It forces a unique ground state and bounds the spectral gap from below by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:intro-purity-gap}
  \Delta_L = E_1(L) - E_0(L) \ge \frac{1}{\beta} \log\frac{\Pt(L, \beta)}{1 - \Pt(L, \beta)}
\end{equation}
(Lemma~\ref{lem:purity-to-gap}). The bound~\eqref{eq:intro-purity-gap} is uniform in \(L\) when the
thermal defect \(1 - \Pt(L, \beta)\) is bounded away from \(1/2\) uniformly in \(L\), and it remains
bounded away from zero as \(\beta\) grows only if the defect decays exponentially in \(\beta\).
Squaring the Gibbs operator in imaginary time improves \(\Pt\) at fixed \(L\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:purity-squaring}), whereas advancing \(L\) and \(\beta\) together via the spacetime
coupling identities (Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling}) requires a spatial transfer operator. If the
nearest-neighbor bond interaction is reflection positive (Definition~\ref{def:rpring}), meaning
\(-h = \sum_\alpha C_\alpha \otimes \overline{C_\alpha}\) in some local basis with a real symmetric
on-site term \(o\), then \(Z_L(\beta)\) admits a spectral moment representation
\(\sum_i \mu_i^L\) of a positive semidefinite transfer operator at even lengths \(L\)
(Definition~\ref{def:moment-form} and Theorem~\ref{thm:rp-moment-bounds}), and both purities
improve quadratically when length and inverse temperature are doubled together
(Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling}). Two purity bounds at a single \emph{seed} scale \((n_0, \beta_0)\) then
yield a uniform lower bound on \(\Delta_L\) for every even \(L \ge n_0\)
(Theorems~\ref{thm:gap-of-seeds} and~\ref{thm:rp-gap}).

\section{Organization of the document}

The exposition is organized into seven chapters following this introduction.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Chapter~\ref{chap:criterion} establishes the abstract spacetime-bootstrap criterion for any
    family of finite spectra whose partition functions satisfy three transfer-operator moment
    inequalities (Definition~\ref{def:moment-bounds} and Theorem~\ref{thm:gap-of-seeds}).
  \item Chapter~\ref{chap:chain} extends the criterion to asymmetric seed defects by iterating the
    decoupled scale-doubling recurrence (Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling-step}) through finite rational
    certificates verified in exact arithmetic (Definition~\ref{def:chain-certificate} and
    Theorem~\ref{thm:gap-of-chain-certificate}).
  \item Chapter~\ref{chap:rings} proves that every reflection-positive spin ring
    (Definition~\ref{def:rpring}) satisfies the moment inequalities by constructing brickwork
    Trotter transfer matrices that are positive semidefinite (Theorem~\ref{thm:psd-transfer}) and
    passing to the continuous-time limit via the Lie--Trotter product formula
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:lie-trotter} and Theorem~\ref{thm:rp-moment-bounds}), yielding the general
    spectral gap criterion for reflection-positive rings (Theorem~\ref{thm:rp-gap}).
  \item Chapter~\ref{chap:heisenberg} applies the ring criterion to the periodic spin-1
    antiferromagnetic Heisenberg chain (Definition~\ref{def:heisenberg}), establishing uniform
    Haldane gap bounds conditional on two finite-size seed inequalities
    (Theorems~\ref{thm:haldane-gap} and~\ref{thm:haldane-gap-sixty}).
  \item Chapter~\ref{chap:open} extends the transfer-operator moment method to open spin-1
    Heisenberg chains with boundary magnetic fields (Theorem~\ref{thm:open-criterion} and
    Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-field-gap}).
  \item Chapter~\ref{chap:models} applies Theorem~\ref{thm:rp-gap} and sitewise unitary conjugation
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:conj-spectrum}) to seven further families of spin-1 and spin-\(\tfrac12\)
    chains and ladders (Theorems~\ref{thm:large-d-gap}, \ref{thm:spin-one-dimerized-gap},
    \ref{thm:two-leg-ising-gap}, \ref{thm:ising-longitudinal-gap}, \ref{thm:staggered-physical-gap},
    \ref{thm:dimerized-physical-gap}, and~\ref{thm:ladder-physical-gap}).
  \item Chapter~\ref{chap:seedkernel} proves the finite-size seed inequalities from first
    principles via Rayleigh--Ritz residual bounds (Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}) and Gershgorin
    comparison (Lemma~\ref{lem:ising-classical}) for the mixed-field Ising chain at
    \((g, h) = (1/4, 1/20)\), discharging Lemma~\ref{lem:ising-longitudinal-seeds} at that point
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:ising-seeds}) and yielding an unconditional spectral gap theorem
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:ising-gap}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Notations and conventions}

Rather than indexing eigenvalues in nondecreasing order, we say that a finite spectrum \(e\) has a
\emph{spectral gap greater than \(c\)} (Definition~\ref{def:gapgt}) if it attains a nondegenerate
minimum at some state and every other eigenvalue lies strictly more than \(c\) above that minimum.
This condition simultaneously expresses ground-state uniqueness and the lower bound
\(E_1 - E_0 > c\) on the spectral gap (Lemma~\ref{lem:gapgt-monotone}). Partition functions of
concrete spin rings are written as \(Z_L(\beta) = \operatorname{Re}\Tr e^{-\beta H_L}\), the real
part of the complex matrix trace (Definition~\ref{def:ring-spectrum} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ring-trace-exp}). Ring lengths \(L\) count unit cells.
