Each entry is a theorem about a periodic chain or ladder: for every even ring length $L \ge L_0$, the Hamiltonian $H_L$ has a unique ground state, and its gap $\Delta_L = E_1(L) - E_0(L)$ is at least the number shown. The proof needs one finite computation, at a seed ring length $n_0$ and inverse temperature $\beta_0$, carried out in exact or interval arithmetic and re-run by the model's checker; the doubling recurrence then covers every larger ring. The last section explains the two columns of bounds.

How the bounds are checked

The column certified gap is computed from the seed by 48 doubling steps in interval arithmetic. The seed itself comes from verified enclosures of the ring spectra, or from exact rational arithmetic, and the model's checker recomputes everything from the stored certificate. The column Lean 4 is the bound proved by a Lean 4 theorem that takes the two seed inequalities at $(n_0, \beta_0)$ as hypotheses and runs 14 exact rational doubling steps; it is a little smaller, typically by about $10^{-5}$. A theorem marked unconditional also proves the seed inequalities inside the Lean kernel, so it rests on nothing outside Lean.

On every change, continuous integration rebuilds the Lean theorems, replays them in the Lean kernel with leanchecker, confirms that they use only Lean's standard axioms, and re-runs the checkers of the models that changed.
