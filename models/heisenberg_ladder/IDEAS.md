# Heisenberg ladder: route analysis

Lengths count rungs (two spin-1/2 sites, $d = 4$ per unit cell). In this document, "threshold" refers to the coupling threshold reached by the finite-size seed grid ($n_0 \le 4$), not the physical gap-closing coupling of the ladder.

## Candidate routes

1. **Closed forms.** At zero leg coupling, $Z_n(\beta) = (e^{3\beta J_\perp/4} + 3e^{-\beta J_\perp/4})^n$ exactly. With non-zero leg coupling, the isotropic ladder is neither commuting nor free-fermionic (Jordan–Wigner transformation leaves four-fermion interactions), so the independent-rung point serves only as a reference limit.
2. **Convergent expansions.** A strong-rung linked-cluster or polymer expansion could bound $\log Z_{2n} - 2\log Z_n$ exponentially in $n$ at a cost determined by the expansion order rather than $n$. However, certifying moderate couplings ($J_\perp \approx 2.5\text{–}4$) requires a proved convergence majorant and wrapping remainder that are not currently implemented.
3. **Transfer operator.** After the checkerboard rotation, all cross-cell bond factors are real, making a Trotterized transfer kernel entrywise positive. However, the transfer-matrix dimension grows exponentially with the number of Trotter slices, and reflection positivity guarantees self-adjointness rather than an explicit Birkhoff contraction rate.
4. **Perturbation from independent rungs.** Applying Weyl's inequality around the independent-rung limit bounds $\log Z_n$ with an error extensive in $n$. While this costs $O(1)$ at any $n$, the extensive error destroys the bulk cancellation between purities. As proved in [`METHOD.md`](METHOD.md), the naive independent-rung norm sandwich **fails for every $n$ and $\beta$ when $0 < J_\perp \le 4$**, even with centered leg bonds. By contrast, the same operator-norm bound is useful *locally in $J_\perp$* to continue a certified seed across a small interval $|J_\perp - J_*| \le \delta$.
5. **Smaller seeds.** The identity $P_\tau(2n, \beta) = P_{\mathrm{s}}(n, 2\beta) P_\tau(n, \beta)^2 / P_{\mathrm{s}}(n, \beta)^2$ is exact, but bounding $P_\tau(2n, \beta)$ via the numerator still requires $P_{\mathrm{s}}(n, 2\beta)$—which depends on $Z_{2n}(2\beta)$—so it does not eliminate the largest ring without an independent bound on $P_{\mathrm{s}}(n, 2\beta)$. Odd seed lengths $n_0$ are not directly covered by the single-cell reflection theorem, so 10-rung traces cannot be used for an $n_0 = 5$ seed without an additional odd-$n_0$ bridge.
6. **Symmetry-resolved complete spectra (chosen).** We work in the unrotated $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ basis (unitarily equivalent to the reflection-positive model) and block-diagonalize by total magnetization, rung momentum, leg exchange, and spin inversion, with exact multiplicities from spin flip and complex conjugation. Verified eigenvalue enclosures (interval matrix entries, residual and orthogonality bounds, and Weyl's inequality) are computed for $2$, $4$, and $8$ rungs, supplemented by exploratory floating-point scans up to $10$ rungs (largest block dimension $8{,}398$).
7. **Variational sandwiches.** Rung-singlet or MPS trial states and Anderson cluster lower bounds control $E_0$, but do not bound the excited-state thermal contributions needed for partition function ratios.

## Practical notes

- Running six 10-rung scans concurrently with dense complex intermediates exceeds 17 GiB of RAM. The implementation therefore constructs symmetry blocks sparsely and maps complex momentum sectors to real symmetric matrices via a sparse antiunitary basis change. Scans should be run one or two at a time.
- The 10-rung exploratory scan outputs are stored in compact form (sector table, $\log Z$ grid, and lowest 32 eigenvalues); full eigenvalue lists can be regenerated via `scripts/scan.jl` and are not required by any checker.

## Outcome

- Certified seeds at $n_0 = 4$ rungs for $J_\perp = 4$, $7/2$, $3$, $5/2$, and $163069/65536$, with local coupling continuation ($\delta = 10^{-4}$) around $J_\perp = 4, 7/2, 3$.
- All 128 grid seeds ($n_0 \in \{2, 4\}$, $\beta_0 = k/4$ for $k = 1, \dots, 64$) are rigorously rejected at $J_\perp = 326133/131072$ using lower defect enclosures and exact recurrence divergence witnesses. At $J_\perp = 2$ and $3/2$, the rejection is extended to all $\beta > 0$ using monotone temperature-window bounds and a low-temperature ground-energy tail.
- Exploratory floating-point scans up to 10 rungs find no admissible even seed at $J_\perp \le 2$. A two-point fit of the ground-energy splitting estimates that $n_0 \approx 6$ ($J_\perp = 2$) and $n_0 \approx 8$ ($J_\perp = 3/2$) would be needed, corresponding to $24$- and $32$-spin rings beyond dense exact diagonalization. Reaching those couplings will require convergent cluster expansions or transfer-operator methods.
