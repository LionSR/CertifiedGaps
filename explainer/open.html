Open chains

Part II of the spacetime bootstrap: why an open spin-1 chain with pinned ends has a unique ground state and a gap, in eleven figures

In Part I, periodic boundary conditions joined the last spin back to the first, turning the partition function into a trace \(\operatorname{Tr}X_\beta^{\,L}\). Now cut the ring open into a chain with two free ends, and apply a small magnetic field to the two endpoint spins. The bulk of the chain is unchanged, so the ring proof carries over with one new ingredient: the open ends of the network are closed by boundary vectors instead of a trace, and the proof must show that those boundary vectors have a nonzero overlap with the dominant eigenvector of \(X_\beta\). II.1Ends carry free spins

Opening the ring changes both the network and the physics. In the network, cutting one bond leaves no loop to close, so \(Z\) is no longer \(\operatorname{Tr}X_\beta^{\,n}\). Physically, cutting a bond leaves a nearly free spin-\(\tfrac12\) at each end of the chain. The valence-bond solid (VBS) picture of Affleck, Kennedy, Lieb, and Tasaki (exact for their AKLT chain and a qualitative model for the Heisenberg chain) shows why: view each spin-1 site as a symmetrized pair of spin-\(\tfrac12\) constituents, and pair neighboring halves across each bond into an antisymmetric spin-\(0\) singlet \((|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2\). On a ring, every spin-\(\tfrac12\) finds a partner; on an open chain, one unpaired spin-\(\tfrac12\) is left over at each end, producing \(2\times2=4\) low-lying states (a singlet and a three-state triplet) whose splitting shrinks as the chain grows.

Exact diagonalization of small chains shows the consequence. Without a boundary field, even chains have three states just above the ground state, at \(0.51, 0.31, 0.20, 0.14, 0.10\) for \(n = 4, \dots, 12\), and odd chains have a three-fold degenerate ground state, so there is no uniform gap. Applying a pinning field \(h=3/5\) along \(z\) to the two end spins, \(-h(S_1^z+S_n^z)\), splits the four edge states: on odd chains (where both ends lie on the same antiferromagnetic sublattice), the ground state is unique and the gap levels off at \(0.72, 0.64, 0.58, 0.55\) for \(n = 5, \dots, 11\).

To see how an open end is closed in a transfer-matrix network, return to the classical Ising chain of \(n\) spins with \(n-1\) bond matrices \(T(s_i,s_{i+1})=e^{Ks_is_{i+1}}\) and an endpoint field weight \(u(s)=e^{hs}\) on \(s_1\) and \(s_n\). The vector \(u\) caps the two free outer legs, turning the partition function into \(Z=u^{\mathsf T}T^{n-1}u=\sum_i\lambda_i^{n-1}\langle\varphi_i,u\rangle^2\). Whereas a periodic trace weights every eigenvalue \(\lambda_i\) by \(1\), an open chain weights \(\lambda_i\) by the squared overlap \(\langle\varphi_i,u\rangle^2\) of the boundary vector \(u\) with the eigenvector \(\varphi_i\). For the classical Ising chain, \(T\) and \(u\) have positive entries, so the top eigenvector \(\varphi_0\) is positive by the Perron–Frobenius theorem and the overlap never vanishes; for the quantum chain, the overlap with the top eigenvector must be proved positive and tracked.

Levels from floating-point exact diagonalization with conserved Sz (n ≤ 12), a diagnostic and not part of the proof. With an odd number of sites both ends lie on the same sublattice, so equal fields suit both.

\[ H_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_{i+1}-h\,(S_1^z+S_n^z),\qquad h=\tfrac35 . \]

Theorem: for every odd \(n\ge1921\) the ground state is unique and \(\Delta_n>\log10/392\approx0.0059\).

II.2A cylinder, not a torus

What replaces the spacetime torus of Section 5 when the chain has two ends? The partition function \(Z=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H_n}\) of the open chain is still a trace over spin states, which glues \(\tau=\beta\) to \(\tau=0\), so imaginary time is still a circle of length \(\beta\). Space, however, is no longer periodic: the chain is a segment of \(n\) sites with two endpoints.

The \(n\times\beta\) spacetime sheet therefore closes in imaginary time only, forming a cylinder with two circular boundary rims. Inside the cylinder, every site still carries the exact same bulk transfer operator \(X_\beta\) as on the ring; the only change is at the two ends, where the row of \(n\) transfer matrices \(X_\beta^{\,n}\) is closed by boundary vectors \(\langle v|\) and \(|v\rangle\) instead of a trace.

\[ \text{ring: }\ \operatorname{Tr}X^n\qquad\text{open chain: }\ \langle v|\,X^n\,|v\rangle . \]

II.3What a boundary vector is

How does an endpoint magnetic field become a vector on bond histories? Each end spin has a bond on its inner side only; on its outer side acts the single-site field term \(\pm hS^z\). When we expand \(e^{-\beta H}\) in imaginary time, a bulk bond fires events that place \(G_\alpha\) on two neighboring sites, whereas the end field acts as half a bond: it fires field events at random imaginary times on the end site's world line alone, each inserting \(G_z=iS^z\) on the end site together with a scalar factor.

That list of field events on the end site's outer leg is a history \(a\), just like the list of events on a bond, and its weight defines a boundary vector on histories: \(v_\beta(a)=(ih)^d\) when all \(d\) events in \(a\) are of type \(z\) (with \(v_\beta(\varnothing)=1\) for zero events), and \(0\) otherwise. Summing \(|v_\beta(a)|^2=h^{2d}\) over ordered times gives \(\|v_\beta\|^2=\sum_{d\ge0}(\beta h^2)^d/d!=e^{\beta h^2}\).

Since \(G_z=iS^z\), an event factor \((ih)G_z=-hS^z\) in \(e^{-\beta H}\) corresponds to adding \(+hS_n^z\) to \(H\) on the right end, while the complex-conjugated factor \((-ih)G_z=+hS^z\) corresponds to adding \(-hS_1^z\) on the left end. Thus, with field \(-h\) on the left end and \(+h\) on the right end (the opposite-field chain \(H_n^{\mathrm{opp}}\), with partition function \(Y_n=\langle v|X^n|v\rangle\)), the two caps carry the same vector \(v\), one conjugated, so every even power \(\langle v|X^{2m}|v\rangle=\|X^mv\|^2\ge0\) is a nonnegative squared norm by the symmetry of \(X_\beta\) from Section 13. The equal-field chain \(H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) of the theorem (\(-hS_1^z\) and \(-hS_n^z\)) is reached by applying a \(\pi\) rotation \(U_P\) about the \(x\)-axis (\(S^z\to-S^z\)) to flip the sign of every \(z\)-event on the right cap, giving \(Y_n^{\mathrm{same}}=\langle v|X^nU_P|v\rangle\).

The left cap is conjugated: its events carry (−ih)Gz = +hSz, adding −hS1z; the right cap adds +hSnz.

\[ v_\beta(a)=(ih)^d\ \ (\text{all labels }z),\qquad \|v_\beta\|^2=e^{\beta h^2},\qquad Y^{\mathrm{same}}_n=\langle v|X^nU_P|v\rangle . \]

II.4The overlap

A periodic trace \(\operatorname{Tr}X^m=\sum_i\lambda_i^m\) always contains the top eigenvalue \(\lambda_0\) of the transfer matrix with weight \(1\); a boundary vector \(v\) might be orthogonal to the top eigenvector \(\psi\), in which case the open chain would never see \(\lambda_0\). To track how strongly \(v\) couples to \(\psi\), divide \(X_\beta\) by its simple positive top eigenvalue \(\lambda_0\) so that the normalized transfer matrix \(T=X_\beta/\lambda_0\) has top eigenvalue \(1\), and split \(T\) into its unit top eigenvector \(\psi\) and the orthogonal remainder \(R\): \(T=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+R\) with \(R|\psi\rangle=0\) and \(\|R\|<1\).

Because \(\psi\) and \(R\) are orthogonal, \(T^m=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+R^m\): repeated application of \(T\) keeps the component of \(v\) along \(\psi\) unchanged while shrinking the perpendicular part by \(\|R\|<1\) at each step. The rescaled opposite-field partition function \(B_m=Y_m/\lambda_0^m=\langle v|T^m|v\rangle\) therefore splits into a length-independent boundary overlap \(c=|\langle\psi,v\rangle|^2\ge0\) plus a remainder \(D_m=\langle v|R^m|v\rangle\) that dies away with the chain length \(m\). The boundary defect \(e_m=D_m/B_m=1-c/B_m\) measures the remainder's share of \(B_m\); certifying \(e_m<1\) proves \(c=(1-e_m)B_m>0\).

\[ B_m=\langle v|T^m|v\rangle=\underbrace{|\langle\psi,v\rangle|^2}_{c}+\underbrace{\langle v|R^m|v\rangle}_{D_m},\qquad e_m=\frac{D_m}{B_m}=1-\frac{c}{B_m}. \]

II.5Why the remainder is a square

The remainder operator \(R\) can have both positive and negative eigenvalues, so why is \(D_m=\langle v|R^m|v\rangle\) nonnegative for even chain lengths \(m\), and how does lengthening the chain shrink the boundary defect \(e_m\)?

For even \(m\), split \(R^m=R^{m/2}R^{m/2}\) and fold the strip of \(m\) boxes at its midpoint. Because \(X_\beta\) is symmetric by reflection positivity, \(R\) is self-adjoint (\(R^\dagger=R\)), so the two halves of the folded strip are conjugate mirror images and \(D_m=\langle R^{m/2}v,R^{m/2}v\rangle=\|R^{m/2}v\|^2\ge0\) is a squared norm.

Now compare an even length \(k\) with a longer even length \(m\ge k\). Absorbing \(k/2\) factors of \(R\) into each end defines the vector \(w=R^{k/2}v\), whose squared norm is \(\|w\|^2=D_k\). This leaves \(m-k\) factors of \(R\) in the middle: \(D_m=\langle w|R^{m-k}|w\rangle\le\|R\|^{m-k}\|w\|^2=\|R\|^{m-k}D_k\). Because \(\|R\|<1\), making the chain longer shrinks the remainder \(D_m\) geometrically by \(\|R\|^{m-k}\) while the overlap \(c\) stays unchanged—which drives the boundary defect \(e_m=D_m/(c+D_m)\) down with length.

\[ D_m=\big\|R^{m/2}v\big\|^2\ge0,\qquad D_m\le\|R\|^{m-k}D_k . \]

II.6Three moves per scale

On a ring, we doubled length by squaring the spatial state \(\sigma_n\); an open chain has no spatial loop, so how can we double its length without losing thermal purity? For a long open chain where the overlap \(c(\beta)\) dominates the remainder, \(Y_m(\beta)\approx c(\beta)\lambda_0(\beta)^m\) is a bulk factor \(\lambda_0(\beta)^m\) per site times a boundary factor \(c(\beta)\) independent of \(m\). Its thermal purity \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(m,\beta)=Y_m(2\beta)/Y_m(\beta)^2\approx[\lambda_0(2\beta)/\lambda_0(\beta)^2]^m\cdot c(2\beta)/c(\beta)^2\) therefore splits into a bulk eigenvalue ratio \(r=\lambda_0(2\beta)/\lambda_0(\beta)^2\approx1\) (already controlled by the periodic ring) times a boundary ratio \(c(2\beta)/c(\beta)^2\) that does not depend on \(m\)—and so cancels when we compare lengths \(M\) and \(2M\).

At each scale \(j\) (with \(\beta_j=49\cdot2^{j-2}\), \(n_j=120\cdot2^{j-2}\), and \(M_j=2n_j\)), the open-chain bootstrap tracks two numbers alongside the ring defect \(\varepsilon_j\): the boundary defect \(e_{M_j/2}(j)\le x\) at half-length \(M_j/2\), and the opposite-field thermal purity \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-x\) at length \(M_j\). Three moves advance both bounds to \((2M_j,\beta_{j+1})\) with defect \(10x^2\):

  1. Overlap recurrence (squeeze the boundary defect at \(\beta_{j+1}\)): At the next temperature \(\beta_{j+1}\), geometric suppression over the \(M_j-M_j/2=M_j/2\) extra sites (\(D_{M_j}'\le q_jD_{M_j/2}'\)) combined with the imaginary-time bridge identity \(B_m(j+1)=P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(m,\beta_j)r_j^{-m}B_m(j)^2\) squeezes the new boundary defect to \(e_{M_j}(j+1)\le10x^2\).
  2. Double the length from \(M_j\) to \(2M_j\): Dividing the bridge identity at \(2M_j\) by the same identity at \(M_j\) cancels \(c_{j+1}\) and bounds \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(2M_j,\beta_j)\ge P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(M_j,\beta_j)\,r_j^{M_j}(1-e_{M_j}(j+1))\ge1-2x-16x^2\), costing very little once the overlap is controlled.
  3. Square the Gibbs state in imaginary time: Stacking two copies of the open-chain Gibbs state \(\rho_{2M_j}^{\mathrm{opp}}(\beta_j)\) doubles \(\beta_j\to\beta_{j+1}\) without needing a ring, and the squaring function \(f\) of Section 10 sends the defect \(2x+16x^2\) below \(10x^2\).

Composition in imaginary time links the scales: Bm(j+1) = Pτopp(m, βj) rj−m Bm(j)2.

\[ e_{M_j/2}(j)\le x,\ \ P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-x\ \Longrightarrow\ e_{M_j}(j{+}1)\le10x^2,\ \ P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(2M_j,\beta_{j+1})\ge1-10x^2 . \]

II.7One open-chain step, in numbers

The clean rule \(x\to10x^2\) holds once all defects are below \(0.01\), whereas the certified starting bounds at scale \(j=2\) (\(\beta_2=49\), \(M_2=240\), \(k_2=142\)) are larger: boundary defect \(E=0.14\) and open-chain thermal defect \(\eta=1-P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}=0.21\), together with the periodic ring defects \(\varepsilon_2=0.052\) and \(\varepsilon_3=6\varepsilon_2^2=0.0162\). Walking through the first step \(j=2\to3\) with every number shown demonstrates how the exact formulas of Moves 1–3 bridge this initial regime.

In Move 1, the periodic ring bound on \(\|R_3\|^{240-142}\) gives the remainder suppression factor \(q=0.142\), so \(q/(1-q)=0.1655\), while the bracket \([1/((1-E)^2(1-\eta))-1]=[1/(0.7396\times0.79)-1]=0.7115\). Multiplying the two gives the new boundary defect bound \(E'\le0.1655\times0.7115=0.118\), below the note's rounded table entry \(0.12\). In Moves 2 and 3, rounding \(E'\) up to \(0.12\) gives a doubled-length purity of at least \((1-\eta)(1-\varepsilon_2)(1-\varepsilon_3)(1-E')=0.79\times0.948\times0.984\times0.88=0.648\) (defect \(0.352\)), and squaring in imaginary time compresses that defect to \(\eta'\le f(0.352)=0.352^2/(2\times0.648^2)=0.147\), below the table entry \(0.155\). Both defects shrink; two more explicit steps (\(j=3\to4\to5\)) reach \(0.001\), after which the rule \(x\to10x^2\) takes over.

II.8Starting numbers

What are the finite-scale inputs that launch the open-chain iteration? The iteration starts at scale \(j=2\) (\(\beta_2=49\)) from four certified numbers: two periodic ring purities \(P_{\mathrm s}(120,49),\,P_\tau(240,49)\ge0.948\) (defect \(\varepsilon_2=0.052\), obtained from the periodic seed of Section 18), and two open-chain bounds from OpenAI's boundary-field paper—the boundary defect \(e_{142}\le0.14\) at length \(142\) and the opposite-field thermal purity \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(240,49)\ge0.79\) at length \(240\) (defect \(\eta_2=0.21\)).

Three explicit rounds of the update rules of Section II.7 (\(j=2\to3\to4\to5\)) bring both open-chain defects down to \(0.001\) at scale \(j=5\), where \(\beta_5=392\) and length \(M_5=1920\). Since \(0.001\) and \(\varepsilon_5\) lie well below \(x_5=0.01=10^{-2}\), the induction rule \(x_{j+1}=10x_j^2\) takes over from \(j=5\) onward and gives \(x_j=10^{-1-2^{j-5}}\)—falling doubly exponentially as \(10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-5}, 10^{-9}, 10^{-17}, \dots\), just as on the ring.

Rows j = 2…5 are the rational table of the note; recomputing the recurrence gives values at or below each entry.

\[ P_{\mathrm s}(120,49),\,P_\tau(240,49)\ge0.948,\quad e_{142}\le0.14,\quad P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(240,49)\ge0.79 . \]

II.9Odd lengths, and the gap

The scale induction controlled the opposite-field chain at even lengths \(M_j=1920\cdot2^{j-5}\); to finish the proof, we must pass to the equal-field chain \(H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) at every odd length \(n\ge1921\). For odd \(n\), \(R^n\) cannot be folded in half into a square because \(R\) can have negative eigenvalues, and the right cap carries the \(\pi\) rotation \(U_P\).

Instead, pick the scale \(j\ge5\) whose even window \([M_j,2M_j]\) contains \(n\), and write \(M=M_j\). By the bulk rotation symmetry, \(U_P\) acts trivially next to the top eigenvector (\(|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|U_P=|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|\)), so the rescaled equal-field partition function \(Q_j(n)=Y_n^{\mathrm{same}}(\beta_j)/\lambda_j^n\) has the exact same leading overlap \(c_j\) as the opposite-field chain. In the remainder \(Q_j(n)-c_j=\langle v_j|R_j^nU_P|v_j\rangle\), move \(M/2\) factors of \(R_j\) onto each end (\(w=R_j^{M/2}v_j\)), leaving \(R_j^{n-M}U_P\) in the middle. Since \(\|R_j^{n-M}U_P\|\le1\), Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the remainder in magnitude by \(|Q_j(n)-c_j|\le\|w\|^2=D_M(j)\), whatever its sign.

This traps \(Q_j(n)\) between \(c_j-D_M(j)\) and \(c_j+D_M(j)\) and bounds the equal-field thermal purity throughout the window \([M_j,2M_j]\) by \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{same}}(n,\beta_j)\ge1-4x_j>\tfrac12\). Because the windows \([M_j,2M_j]\) cover every \(n\ge1920\), for every odd \(n\ge1921\) the ground state holds weight \(w_0\ge1-4x_j>\tfrac12\) and is therefore unique, and the weight ratio \(e^{-\beta_j\Delta_n}=w_1/w_0\le4x_j/(1-4x_j)<10^{-2^{j-5}}\) at \(\beta_j=392\cdot2^{j-5}\) gives \(\Delta_n>\log10/392\approx0.0059\).

\[ e^{-\beta_j\Delta_n}\le\frac{4x_j}{1-4x_j}<10^{-2^{j-5}},\quad\beta_j=392\cdot2^{j-5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta_n>\frac{\log10}{392}. \]
