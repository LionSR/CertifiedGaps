Open chains

Part II of the spacetime bootstrap: how boundary fields and an overlap recurrence establish a uniform spectral gap for open spin-1 chains

In Part I, periodic boundary conditions joined the last spin back to the first and expressed the partition function as a trace \(\operatorname{Tr}X_\beta^{\,L}\). Cutting the ring into an open chain with two free ends and applying a pinning field \(h=3/5\) to the two end spins leaves the bulk transfer operator \(X_\beta\) unchanged, while closing the open ends of the network with boundary vectors instead of a trace. To extend the ring proof to open chains, we must show that these boundary vectors have a strictly positive overlap with the dominant eigenvector of \(X_\beta\).

As on the ring, a thermal purity close to \(1\) forces a spectral gap: for the open chain with equal end fields, \(e^{-\beta\Delta_n}\le(1-P_\tau^{\mathrm{same}})/P_\tau^{\mathrm{same}}\). The proof bounds this purity along a sequence of scales where length and inverse temperature double together, \(\beta_j=49\cdot2^{j-2}\), ring length \(n_j=120\cdot2^{j-2}\), and open-chain length \(M_j=2n_j\). At every scale \(j\ge5\), it maintains three bounds with a common defect bound \(x_j\), where \(x_5=0.01\) and \(x_{j+1}=10x_j^2\), so \(x_j=10^{-1-2^{j-5}}\):

  • (R)Bulk transfer-operator dominance: the periodic ring purity defect \(\varepsilon_j\) is at most \(x_j\), so \(X_{\beta_j}\) has a single dominant eigenvalue (Sections II.7–II.8).
  • (E)Boundary overlap control: the boundary defect \(e_{k_j}(j)\) at length \(k_j=M_j/2\) (the fraction of the open-chain partition function orthogonal to the leading eigenvector) is at most \(x_j\) (Sections II.3–II.6).
  • (T)Open-chain thermal purity: the thermal defect \(1-P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(M_j,\beta_j)\) of the auxiliary chain with opposite end fields is at most \(x_j\) (Section II.6).

Together, these three bounds yield \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{same}}(n,\beta_j)\ge1-4x_j\) for every length \(M_j\le n\le2M_j\) (Section II.9), which at \(\beta_j=392\cdot2^{j-5}\) gives \(\Delta_n>\log10/392\). Bound (R) depends only on the periodic ring. Bounds (E) and (T) at scale \(j+1\) follow from (E) and (T) at scale \(j\) combined with (R) (Section II.6), and Section II.8 establishes the base case \(j=5\) from the four finite-scale seed inputs at \(j=2\). Because the intervals \([M_j,2M_j]\) for \(j\ge5\) cover all lengths \(n\ge M_5=1920\), the gap bound holds for every odd length \(n\ge1921\).

II.1Ends carry free spins

Cutting a periodic spin-1 ring into an open chain changes both the tensor network and the low-energy spectrum. In the network, removing one bond leaves no spatial loop to close, so \(Z\) is no longer \(\operatorname{Tr}X_\beta^{\,n}\). Physically, cutting a bond leaves an effective spin-\(\tfrac12\) degree of freedom at each end of the chain. In the Affleck–Kennedy–Lieb–Tasaki (AKLT) valence-bond picture, each spin-1 site is a symmetrized pair of spin-\(\tfrac12\) constituents, and neighboring constituents across each bond lock into an antisymmetric spin-\(0\) singlet \((|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2\). On a ring, every spin-\(\tfrac12\) is paired; on an open chain, one unpaired spin-\(\tfrac12\) remains at each boundary, producing \(2\times2=4\) low-lying states (a singlet and a triplet) whose energy splitting decays exponentially with chain length.

Exact diagonalization of small chains illustrates this boundary degeneracy. Without a boundary field, even-length chains have three triplet states just above the singlet ground state, with excitation energy \(0.51, 0.31, 0.20, 0.14, 0.10\) for \(n = 4, \dots, 12\), and odd-length chains have a three-fold degenerate ground state, so there is no uniform spectral gap. Applying a pinning field \(h=3/5\) along \(z\) to the two end spins, \(-h(S_1^z+S_n^z)\), lifts this degeneracy: on odd-length chains (where both endpoints lie on the same antiferromagnetic sublattice), the ground state is unique and the gap stabilizes at \(0.72, 0.64, 0.58, 0.55\) for \(n = 5, \dots, 11\).

In a transfer-matrix network, open endpoints are closed by boundary vectors. For a classical Ising chain of \(n\) spins with \(n-1\) bond matrices \(T(s_i,s_{i+1})=e^{Ks_is_{i+1}}\) and an endpoint field weight \(u(s)=e^{hs}\) on \(s_1\) and \(s_n\), the vector \(u\) caps the two free outer legs, giving \(Z=u^{\mathsf T}T^{n-1}u=\sum_i\lambda_i^{n-1}\langle\varphi_i,u\rangle^2\). Whereas a periodic trace weights every eigenvalue \(\lambda_i\) by \(1\), an open chain weights \(\lambda_i\) by the squared overlap \(\langle\varphi_i,u\rangle^2\) of the boundary vector \(u\) with the eigenvector \(\varphi_i\). For the classical Ising chain, \(T\) and \(u\) have positive entries, so the top eigenvector \(\varphi_0\) is positive by the Perron–Frobenius theorem and the overlap is automatically positive; for the quantum spin chain, a positive overlap with the top eigenvector must be certified and propagated across scales.

II.2A cylinder, not a torus

For an open chain of \(n\) sites, the partition function \(Z=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H_n}\) is still a trace over spin states, which identifies \(\tau=\beta\) with \(\tau=0\) so that imaginary time remains a circle of circumference \(\beta\). Space, however, is a segment of \(n\) sites with two endpoints rather than a closed ring.

The \(n\times\beta\) spacetime worldsheet therefore closes in imaginary time only, forming a cylinder with two circular boundary rims instead of the torus of Section 5. Inside the cylinder, every site carries the same bulk transfer operator \(X_\beta\) as on the periodic ring; the only change is at the two boundaries, where the product \(X_\beta^{\,n}\) is contracted with boundary vectors \(\langle v|\) and \(|v\rangle\) instead of a trace.

\[ \text{ring: }\ \operatorname{Tr}X^n\qquad\text{open chain: }\ \langle v|\,X^n\,|v\rangle . \]

II.3Boundary vectors

In the Poisson bond-history expansion of \(e^{-\beta H}\), an endpoint magnetic field defines a vector on bond histories. Each end spin has a bulk bond on its inner side and a single-site field term \(\pm hS^z\) on its outer side. Whereas a bulk bond generates Poisson events that act with \(G_\alpha\) on two neighboring sites, the boundary field generates field events at random imaginary times on the end site's worldline alone, each inserting \(G_z=iS^z\) on the end site with a scalar weight.

The sequence of field events on the end site's outer leg forms a history \(a\) on the same state space as a bulk bond, with weight \(v_\beta(a)=(ih)^d\) when all \(d\) events in \(a\) have label \(z\) (and \(v_\beta(\varnothing)=1\) for zero events), and \(0\) otherwise. Integrating \(|v_\beta(a)|^2=h^{2d}\) over ordered event times in \([0,\beta]\) gives \(\|v_\beta\|^2=\sum_{d\ge0}(\beta h^2)^d/d!=e^{\beta h^2}\).

Because \(G_z=iS^z\), an event factor \((ih)G_z=-hS^z\) in \(e^{-\beta H}\) corresponds to a boundary term \(+hS_n^z\) in \(H\) at the right end, whereas the complex-conjugated factor \((-ih)G_z=+hS^z\) corresponds to \(-hS_1^z\) at the left end. Thus, for the opposite-field chain \(H_n^{\mathrm{opp}}\) (with \(-hS_1^z\) on the left and \(+hS_n^z\) on the right, and partition function \(Y_n=\langle v|X^n|v\rangle\)), the two boundaries carry the same vector \(v\) and its conjugate \(\langle v|\). By the self-adjointness of \(X_\beta\) (Section 13), every even length has a nonnegative partition function \(\langle v|X^{2m}|v\rangle=\|X^mv\|^2\ge0\). The equal-field chain \(H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) of the theorem (\(-hS_1^z\) and \(-hS_n^z\)) is obtained by applying a \(\pi\) rotation \(U_P\) about the \(x\)-axis (\(S^z\to-S^z\)) to flip the sign of every \(z\)-event on the right boundary, giving \(Y_n^{\mathrm{same}}=\langle v|X^nU_P|v\rangle\).

The left boundary vector is conjugated: its events carry (−ih)Gz = +hSz in e−βH, corresponding to −hS1z in H, while the right boundary corresponds to +hSnz.

\[ v_\beta(a)=(ih)^d\ \ (\text{all labels }z),\qquad \|v_\beta\|^2=e^{\beta h^2},\qquad Y^{\mathrm{same}}_n=\langle v|X^nU_P|v\rangle . \]

II.4The boundary overlap

A periodic trace \(\operatorname{Tr}X^m=\sum_i\lambda_i^m\) always includes the top eigenvalue \(\lambda_0\) of the transfer operator with unit multiplicity, whereas an open-chain contraction \(\langle v|X^m|v\rangle\) weights \(\lambda_0^m\) by \(|\langle\psi,v\rangle|^2\), which would vanish if \(v\) were orthogonal to the top eigenvector \(\psi\). To track how strongly \(v\) couples to \(\psi\), divide \(X_\beta\) by its simple positive top eigenvalue \(\lambda_0\) so that the normalized transfer operator \(T=X_\beta/\lambda_0\) has top eigenvalue \(1\), and decompose \(T\) into the rank-one projector onto its unit top eigenvector \(\psi\) plus the orthogonal remainder \(R\): \(T=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+R\) with \(R|\psi\rangle=0\) and \(\|R\|<1\).

Because \(\psi\) and \(R\) are orthogonal, \(T^m=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+R^m\): repeated application of \(T\) preserves the component of \(v\) along \(\psi\) while contracting the orthogonal component by at most \(\|R\|<1\) per site. The rescaled opposite-field partition function \(B_m=Y_m/\lambda_0^m=\langle v|T^m|v\rangle\) therefore decomposes into a length-independent boundary overlap \(c=|\langle\psi,v\rangle|^2\ge0\) plus a remainder \(D_m=\langle v|R^m|v\rangle\) that decays with chain length \(m\). The boundary defect \(e_m=D_m/B_m=1-c/B_m\) measures the remainder's fraction of \(B_m\); certifying \(e_m<1\) proves \(c=(1-e_m)B_m>0\).

\[ B_m=\langle v|T^m|v\rangle=\underbrace{|\langle\psi,v\rangle|^2}_{c}+\underbrace{\langle v|R^m|v\rangle}_{D_m},\qquad e_m=\frac{D_m}{B_m}=1-\frac{c}{B_m}. \]

II.5Remainder positivity and decay

Although the remainder operator \(R\) can have both positive and negative eigenvalues, its expectation value \(D_m=\langle v|R^m|v\rangle\) at any even chain length \(m\) is nonnegative and decays geometrically with \(m\).

For even \(m\), factor \(R^m=R^{m/2}R^{m/2}\) and let each half act on one boundary vector. Because \(X_\beta\) is self-adjoint by reflection positivity, \(R\) is also self-adjoint (\(R^\dagger=R\)), so \(D_m=\langle R^{m/2}v,R^{m/2}v\rangle=\|R^{m/2}v\|^2\ge0\) is a squared norm.

Comparing an even length \(k\) with a longer even length \(m\ge k\) gives a geometric decay bound. Absorbing \(k/2\) factors of \(R\) into each boundary defines the vector \(w=R^{k/2}v\) with squared norm \(\|w\|^2=D_k\), leaving \(m-k\) factors of \(R\) in the middle: \(D_m=\langle w|R^{m-k}|w\rangle\le\|R\|^{m-k}\|w\|^2=\|R\|^{m-k}D_k\). Because \(\|R\|<1\), increasing the chain length from \(k\) to \(m\) suppresses the remainder \(D_m\) by at least \(\|R\|^{m-k}\) while leaving the leading overlap \(c\) unchanged, driving down the boundary defect \(e_m=D_m/(c+D_m)\) from one scale to the next.

\[ D_m=\big\|R^{m/2}v\big\|^2\ge0,\qquad D_m\le\|R\|^{m-k}D_k . \]

II.6Three moves per scale

Unlike a periodic ring, an open chain has no spatial loop, so doubling its length cannot be written directly as squaring a spatial operator. Instead, when the leading overlap \(c(\beta)\) dominates the remainder, \(Y_m(\beta)\approx c(\beta)\lambda_0(\beta)^m\) factors into a bulk contribution \(\lambda_0(\beta)^m\) and an \(m\)-independent boundary factor \(c(\beta)\). The opposite-field thermal purity \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(m,\beta)=Y_m(2\beta)/Y_m(\beta)^2\approx[\lambda_0(2\beta)/\lambda_0(\beta)^2]^m\cdot c(2\beta)/c(\beta)^2\) therefore splits into the bulk eigenvalue ratio \(r=\lambda_0(2\beta)/\lambda_0(\beta)^2\approx1\) (already bounded by the periodic ring recurrence) times a boundary ratio \(c(2\beta)/c(\beta)^2\) that is independent of \(m\) and cancels when comparing lengths \(M\) and \(2M\).

At each scale \(j\) (with \(\beta_j=49\cdot2^{j-2}\), \(n_j=120\cdot2^{j-2}\), and \(M_j=2n_j\)), the open-chain recurrence tracks two quantities alongside the periodic ring defect \(\varepsilon_j\) of (R): the boundary defect \(e_{k_j}(j)\le x\) at length \(k_j=M_j/2\) [(E)], and the opposite-field thermal purity \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-x\) at length \(M_j\) [(T)]. Three steps advance both bounds to \((2M_j,\beta_{j+1})\) with defect \(10x^2\):

  1. Overlap recurrence (contract the boundary defect at \(\beta_{j+1}\)): At inverse temperature \(\beta_{j+1}\), geometric decay over the \(M_j-M_j/2=M_j/2\) extra sites (\(D_{M_j}'\le q_jD_{M_j/2}'\)) combined with the imaginary-time bridge identity \(B_m(j+1)=P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(m,\beta_j)r_j^{-m}B_m(j)^2\) contracts the new boundary defect to \(e_{M_j}(j+1)\le10x^2\).
  2. Double the length from \(M_j\) to \(2M_j\): Dividing the bridge identity at \(2M_j\) by the same identity at \(M_j\) cancels \(c_{j+1}\) and yields \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(2M_j,\beta_j)\ge P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(M_j,\beta_j)\,r_j^{M_j}(1-e_{M_j}(j+1))\ge1-2x-16x^2\).
  3. Square the Gibbs state in imaginary time: Squaring the open-chain Gibbs state \(\rho_{2M_j}^{\mathrm{opp}}(\beta_j)\) doubles \(\beta_j\to\beta_{j+1}\), and the thermal squaring map \(f\) of Section 10 compresses the defect \(2x+16x^2\) below \(10x^2\).

Composition in imaginary time links adjacent scales: Bm(j+1) = Pτopp(m, βj) rj−m Bm(j)2.

\[ e_{M_j/2}(j)\le x,\ \ P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(M_j,\beta_j)\ge1-x\ \Longrightarrow\ e_{M_j}(j{+}1)\le10x^2,\ \ P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(2M_j,\beta_{j+1})\ge1-10x^2 . \]

II.7Worked step from j = 2 to j = 3

The simplified asymptotic map \(x\to10x^2\) applies once all defects lie below \(0.01\). At the seed scale \(j=2\) (\(\beta_2=49\), \(M_2=240\), \(k_2=142\)), the initial defects are larger: the boundary defect is \(E=0.14\) and the open-chain thermal defect is \(\eta=1-P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}=0.21\), alongside the periodic ring defects \(\varepsilon_2=0.052\) and \(\varepsilon_3=6\varepsilon_2^2=0.0162\). Evaluating the exact rational recurrence formulas of Moves 1–3 on the step \(j=2\to3\) bridges this initial regime.

In Move 1, the periodic ring bound on \(\|R_3\|^{240-142}\) gives the remainder suppression factor \(q=0.142\), so \(q/(1-q)=0.1655\), while the factor \([1/((1-E)^2(1-\eta))-1]=[1/(0.7396\times0.79)-1]=0.7115\). Multiplying the two bounds the new boundary defect by \(E'\le0.1655\times0.7115=0.118\), below the rounded table entry \(0.12\). In Moves 2 and 3, using \(E'\le0.12\) gives a doubled-length purity of at least \((1-\eta)(1-\varepsilon_2)(1-\varepsilon_3)(1-E')=0.79\times0.948\times0.984\times0.88=0.648\) (defect \(0.352\)), and squaring in imaginary time compresses that defect to \(\eta'\le f(0.352)=0.352^2/(2\times0.648^2)=0.147\), below the table entry \(0.155\). Two further explicit steps (\(j=3\to4\to5\)) bring both defects below \(0.001\), entering the regime where \(x\to10x^2\) applies.

II.8Seed certificate for the open chain

The open-chain recurrence is initialized at scale \(j=2\) (\(\beta_2=49\)) from four seed bounds established in OpenAI’s boundary-field preprint via periodic variational estimates and small-cluster boundary computations. Two are the periodic ring purities \(P_{\mathrm s}(120,49),\,P_\tau(240,49)\ge0.948\), giving the ring defect \(\varepsilon_2=0.052\) for (R). The other two are open-chain seed bounds: the boundary defect \(e_{142}\le0.14\) at length \(k_2=142\) for (E), and the opposite-field thermal purity \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{opp}}(240,49)\ge0.79\) at length \(M_2=240\) (defect \(\eta_2=0.21\)) for (T).

Three explicit steps of the recurrence of Section II.7 (\(j=2\to3\to4\to5\)) reduce both open-chain defects to at most \(0.001\) at scale \(j=5\) (\(\beta_5=392\), \(M_5=1920\)). Because \(0.001\) and \(\varepsilon_5\) lie below \(x_5=0.01=10^{-2}\), this establishes the base case of (E) and (T) at \(j=5\). From \(j=5\) onward, the recurrence \(x_{j+1}=10x_j^2\) gives \(x_j=10^{-1-2^{j-5}}\), which decays doubly exponentially as \(10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-5}, 10^{-9}, 10^{-17}, \dots\). II.9Odd lengths and the gap

The scale recurrence controls the opposite-field chain at even lengths \(M_j=1920\cdot2^{j-5}\). To obtain the spectral gap of the equal-field chain \(H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) at every odd length \(n\ge1921\), we must bound its remainder at odd \(n\), where \(R^n\) cannot be factored into two equal halves and the right boundary carries the \(\pi\) rotation \(U_P\).

Given any odd \(n\ge1921\), choose the scale \(j\ge5\) whose window \([M_j,2M_j]\) contains \(n\), and set \(M=M_j\). By bulk \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\) invariance, \(U_P\) acts trivially on the singlet top eigenvector (\(|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|U_P=|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|\)), so the rescaled equal-field partition function \(Q_j(n)=Y_n^{\mathrm{same}}(\beta_j)/\lambda_j^n\) has the same leading overlap \(c_j\) as the opposite-field chain. In the remainder \(Q_j(n)-c_j=\langle v_j|R_j^nU_P|v_j\rangle\), absorbing \(M/2\) factors of \(R_j\) into each boundary defines \(w=R_j^{M/2}v_j\) and leaves \(R_j^{n-M}U_P\) in the middle. Since \(\|R_j^{n-M}U_P\|\le1\), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality bounds the remainder in magnitude by \(|Q_j(n)-c_j|\le\|w\|^2=D_M(j)\), regardless of its sign.

This encloses \(Q_j(n)\) in \([c_j-D_M(j),\,c_j+D_M(j)]\) and implies \(P_\tau^{\mathrm{same}}(n,\beta_j)\ge1-4x_j>\tfrac12\) throughout the window \([M_j,2M_j]\). Because the windows \([M_j,2M_j]\) cover every \(n\ge1920\), at every odd \(n\ge1921\) the ground state carries thermal weight \(w_0\ge1-4x_j>\tfrac12\) and is therefore unique, and the weight ratio \(e^{-\beta_j\Delta_n}=w_1/w_0\le4x_j/(1-4x_j)<10^{-2^{j-5}}\) at \(\beta_j=392\cdot2^{j-5}\) yields \(\Delta_n>\log10/392\approx0.0059\).

\[ e^{-\beta_j\Delta_n}\le\frac{4x_j}{1-4x_j}<10^{-2^{j-5}},\quad\beta_j=392\cdot2^{j-5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta_n>\frac{\log10}{392}. \]
